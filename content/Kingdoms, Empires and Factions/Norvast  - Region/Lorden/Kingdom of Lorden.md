## Architecture&Environment
The houses of Lorden are built primarily of wood or stone, varying by region and available materials.

**Thangard, the Capital** The capital is constructed largely of ancient stone, its keeps and prominent houses improved over generations by Arcanists. This constant building and rebuilding has resulted in newer streets layered atop older ones, leaving a maze of crannies, forgotten passages, and hidden dwellings beneath the city. These spaces have become ideal breeding grounds for crime.

**The Northern Reaches** In the northern territories, houses are built with timber frames, thick wattle and daub walls, and heavily thatched roofs to endure harsh winters.

**The Mid-Northern Tundras** Where timber grows scarce, construction relies on thick peat turf blocks for walls, stone foundations, and animal hides stretched over frames of whalebone or driftwood depending on proximity to the coast.

**The Southern Grasslands** In the warmer southern regions, houses use locally sourced materials. Timber frames of green oak provide the structural skeleton, with walls of wattle and daub made from woven hazel packed with clay or mud. Steeply pitched roofs covered in thatch of long straw, reeds, or heather allow water to run off easily.

**Common Practices** Throughout Lorden, builders prefer stone or rubble-filled trenches for foundations, keeping wooden elements raised off the damp ground to prevent rot.

## Religion
In Lorden, there are two prevalent religions. One is the faith of the Alderbeasts, which centers on belief in the local guardians of different areas. Folk who travel from town to town or from region to region believe that the local Magbeast that controls an area can protect them and grant blessings. It is possible to tell which Alderbeast belongs to a given region or forest by its shrine, which usually depicts a stone statue of the Magbeast. These shrines are kept by local Wisewomen or Cunningfolk.

Additionally, these Alderbeasts at times have different preferences and customs that vary from village to village, from one village requiring offerings of apples to another that gives tributes only of hay. There are many tales of Alderbeasts protecting villages under attack by raiders, with stories dating back to the days of Silfrkell attacking the inner parts of Lorden.

Additionally, many of the Wisewomen and wild witches of Norvast use the bones of lesser animals to read the omens left by the Alderbeasts, with a strong belief that they are somehow connected to the Colossi.

Alongside this faith exists the [[Religions/Religion - The Myrkvargr Covenant/The Myrkvargr Covenant \|The Myrkvargr Covenant]], a religion that reveres the Alderbeast of the black wolf of Lorden. It is believed to have accompanied the folk of Norvast since ages past, protecting them even in their most dire times. 

These two prevalent religions are similar in aspect yet differ greatly in scale and reverence. They are the [[Religions/Religion - The Myrkvargr Covenant/The Myrkvargr Covenant \|The Myrkvargr Covenant]] and the [[Religions/Religion - Alderdyr/Alderdyr\|Alderdyr]] faith.

## Economy

Lorden uses an older coin system predating Ravania's currency. The kingdom's wealth flows primarily from its natural resources: timber from the vast forests, furs, hides, and leather goods from hunters and trappers, iron ore from the Aldmont mountains, salted fish and whale products from the northern coasts, wool and textiles from southern flocks, and renowned mead, ale, and spirits from Lorden's breweries. Northern craftsmen produce carved bone and ivory goods prized across the region, while amber collected from northern shores fetches high prices in foreign markets. Myrlith ore remains extremely limited but occasionally trades for extraordinary prices when available.

Trade with Ravania is strained due to political tensions, conducted mostly through neutral intermediaries rather than direct exchange. Strong trade ties exist with Tannen by sea, despite occasional piracy issues complicating shipments. Trade with Silfrkell is complicated by their raiding history but persists through specific neutral ports where old enmities are set aside for commerce. Mordantis supplies rare alchemical ingredients in exchange for Lorden's sturdier processed timber. Malten serves as a valued neutral trading partner, facilitating goods between Lorden and the eastern kingdoms. Wyldric trades exotic woods, dyes, and gold for Lorden furs and whale oil.

### What Lorden Produces

Lorden exports timber from its vast forests, furs, hides, and leather goods, iron ore from the Aldmont mountains, salted fish and whale products from the coast, wool and textiles from southern flocks, mead, ale, and spirits, amber from northern shores, and bone and ivory carvings.

### What Lorden Imports

Lorden imports exotic spices and dyes from southern kingdoms, fine wines from the Weinost kingdoms , silk and luxury fabrics, alchemical ingredients not native to the north from Tannen and Mordantis, and gold through intermediaries originating from Ravania and at times Wyldric.

**Currency**
The currency of Norvast uses three types of coins: the Ort, the Grå, and the Marka.

**Ort** - A thin coin of simple iron with a dark grey finish. One side bears the symbol of a sword, the other an arrow. The most common currency used in daily transactions among common folk.

**Gra** - A silver coin, thicker and shinier than the Ort. One side displays a simple star, the other the figure of a man. Used for larger purchases and trade.

**Marka** - A golden coin bearing a crown on one side and a wolf on the other. Reserved for significant transactions, noble dealings, and substantial wealth.

## Culture

### Demographics

Lorden is overwhelmingly human, with the Lorins being the dominant ethnicity descended from the old tribes that once roamed the region. Small pockets of Valk refugees integrated over generations have become indistinguishable from native Lorins, their origins remembered only in family names and old stories. Gigamar populations exist in the mountainous regions, mostly keeping to themselves in remote settlements.

Demiliss are rare in Lorden, usually dwelling in the wilds far from human habitation. Those few who live among humans hide their nature carefully. Half-elves are extremely rare and often viewed with suspicion due to the elven association with foreign magic. Foreign merchants from Tannen, Mordantis, and even Ravania can be found in major trading towns, though they remain outsiders regardless of how long they stay.


#### Treatment of Non-Humans

Gigamar are tolerated in Lorden, seen as useful laborers but kept at arm's length by most communities. Demiliss are distrusted and often blamed for misfortune if their nature is discovered. Foreigners are treated fairly in matters of trade but are rarely granted land or titles regardless of their wealth or service. Thralls still exist in Lorden, though the practice is declining, viewed as old-fashioned by some and distasteful by others.

#### Education

Literacy in Lorden is limited mostly to nobility, clergy, and merchants. Children learn trades through apprenticeship under masters in their craft. Noble children are tutored privately or sent to other households to learn arms, etiquette, and governance. The Astralegis trains those with magical potential, identifying candidates through tests administered across the kingdom. Sagas and oral history are written down in great books but also taught orally by elders to preserve the culture among those who cannot read.

#### Marriage

Arranged marriages are common among nobility for political alliance, binding houses together through blood. Dowries are expected, with land or livestock exchanged among common folk. Ceremonies are performed by Grey Shepherds of the Myrkvargr faith or by local Wisewomen following older Alderdyr traditions. Marriages between different social ranks are rare but not forbidden.

#### Funeral Rites

Myrk Knights receive elaborate rites, with their Myrsword either interred with them or passed on to a worthy successor. The poor are buried in common ground blessed by Grey Shepherds. Some northern regions practice ship burials for important figures, setting the vessel ablaze upon the water. Throughout Lorden, it is believed that improper burial leaves the dead restless, their spirits wandering until the rites are properly performed.
#### Legal System

territorial and bigger disputes are handled by the Baron or Earl of the region. higher serious crimes are judged by the Earl and Baron.
Local disputes are dealt with by a bailiff appointed for such matters. Trial by combat remains legal for disputes of honor, allowing the accused to stake their innocence on their sword arm albeit highly rare and more apparent in the northern rural parts. Witnesses and oaths carry significant weight in proceedings, with oath-breaking considered among the gravest offenses.

Punishments vary by severity. Fines, punishments such as lashing and pillory deal with most offenses, compensating victims or their families. Indentured servitude repays debts or compensates victims when coin is lacking. Exile is reserved for crimes not warranting death but too severe for fines alone. Some crimes carry thralldom, reducing the offender to bondage. Appeals to the King are possible but rarely granted, reserved for matters of great importance or noble status.
### Power Structure
King - Thanaric line ruler, but power balanced by nobles, Thanaric bloodline rules but must maintain support of powerful clans and jarls.

Crown Prince - Designated heir, often co-rules or governs specific regions to gain experience.

Councilors - Small advisory body of the most powerful nobles, high clergy, and the Magisters

#### High Nobility
Usually from old tribe chief houses from the days of old.

**Margrave** - Rules a march (border territory), particularly the southern border facing Ravania, Mordantis and Aldergard. Equal or slightly higher rank than regular Earls due to military importance. Has more military autonomy - can raise levies and respond to threats without waiting for royal approval. Expected to maintain constant military readiness. other Margraves towards Fiorelle, Malten and Ruhmald and others although carrying the same rank are not as highly regarded as that of the southern Margrave.

Jarl and Counts - Rules a large territory (Earldom). Many earls descend from the original tribal chieftains who swore fealty to Siegrick I. They maintain significant autonomy and their own military forces. Some particularly powerful earls may hold the title **High Earl** for the largest territories.

**Baron** - Either Lesser or Higher nobility(depends on land and size usually) leader who serves under an Earl. Commands a warband and administers smaller territories. Holds a Barony.
#### Lesser Nobility 
**Magister** - Governor of a chartered town or city. Elected by guild masters and leading citizens, or appointed by the local Earl/King.

**Landed Knight**/**Thane** - Knights who hold land directly from the crown or from Earls. Most Thanes and Landed Knights trace their blood to ancient Lorin families, their lineages reaching back to the tribes that first emerged from the Aldemont mountains. Some, however, descend from the Valk refugees who integrated into Lorden generations ago. These knights of Valk blood have proven themselves through service and been granted land, though older noble houses occasionally look down upon them for lacking the ancient Lorin heritage. Regardless of origin, Landed Knights serve the King or great lords directly and can rise to significant power through deeds and loyalty.

**Errant Knights** - A landless knight who wanders seeking glory, adventure, and worthy causes. Unlike Knight Bachelors (who serve in a lord's household), Errant Knights have no fixed lord or oath of service.

**Arcanists** - Practitioners of "tamed magic" from Siegrick II's lineage.  while some do come from nobility Not quite nobles, but highly respected. Organized in a loose order/guild structure. Some serve in the **Auxiliary Brigade** (magical military unit). although not always of Nobility, Arcanists are given the respect of Lesser nobility, as their abilities give them recognition. 

#### Commonfolk

**Guild Master** - In the few towns/ports (like on Verdigris), guild masters hold significant local power, especially in trade cities.

**Craftsmen/Merchants -** creates goods and services to sell and trade in regulation of local guilds and rule.

**Yeoman** - Free peasant farmers who own their land. Unlike serfdom in other kingdoms, Lorden maintains a strong yeoman class They owe military service and taxes but cannot be bought/sold.

**Freemen** - Men and women that aren't bound to any land or own their own, however is are still part of the kingdom of Lorden, usually sailors, traveling professionals and other unique status of folk.

**Serf&Bondsman** - Bound to land but not slaves. Less common in Lorden due to strong freeman tradition, but exists in some regions.

**Wisewoman/Cunning Folk** - Rare and few practitioners of older "wild magic" traditions. Viewed with suspicion after the witch hunts but some survive in remote areas. Not officially recognized.

**Thrall/Slaves** - Slaves, though Vallard IX freed the Valk refugees, reducing this class significantly. Remaining thralls are usually war captives from conflicts with Ravania or criminals and occasional Demiliss or half-elves.

## Foreign Relations

### With Ravania

A cold peace exists between Lorden and Ravania, maintained by mutual exhaustion from the last war a century ago. Diplomatic tension simmers beneath the surface, fueled by Ravanian claims that Lorden harbors dangerous magic users and refuses to suppress wild magic practices. Ravanian spies are a constant concern, with Lorden's court deeply suspicious of southern visitors. Trade continues despite political hostility, mostly through neutral intermediaries.

### With Aldergard

Lorden and Aldergard are uneasy neighbors who share a mutual distrust of Ravania but disagree sharply on religious matters. Aldergard missionaries sometimes enter Lorden, causing friction with Alderdyr followers who view their proselytizing as an affront to the Alderbeasts. Occasional border disputes over mountain territories flare up but rarely escalate to open conflict.

### With Mordantis

Relations with Mordantis are friendly, based on trade and a shared wariness of Ravania. Some Lorden nobles distrust Mordantis due to their elven bloodlines, viewing such heritage with suspicion. Mordantis serves as a buffer state between Lorden and potential threats from the south, and Lorden prefers it remain independent rather than fall under Ravanian influence.

### With Tannen

A historic rivalry exists with Tannen over sea trade routes and fishing waters. Occasional naval skirmishes occur but no open war has broken out in living memory. Cultural exchange flows through coastal trading towns where merchants and sailors mingle. Both kingdoms share concern over pirates operating from Verdigris, a problem neither has fully resolved.

### With Silfrkell

The Silfrkell are ancient enemies, their raids remembered in Lorden sagas sung to this day. Current relations are cold but peaceful, with occasional trade conducted through neutral ports. Some Lorden coastal villages still maintain blood feuds with specific Silfrkell clans, passed down through generations. Despite this history, respect exists between warriors on both sides, each recognizing the other's martial prowess.

### With Malten

Malten serves as a neutral trading partner valued for access to eastern goods. Lorden maintains a diplomatic presence there to counter Ravanian and Ruhmald influence. The two powers share responsibility for maintaining the ancient wall against eastern monsters, a duty that binds them despite other differences. Tension exists over Malten being pressured by Ruhmald to align with Ravania, a prospect Lorden watches with concern.

## History&Founding of Lorden
based on the [[Kingdoms, Empires and Factions/Norvast  - Region/Lorden/History of the Monarchy of Lorden\|history of kings.]]
### Origins in the Deep Mountains

The ancestors of the Lorins dwelt deep within the caverns of the Aldemont Veiliss mountains during the Age of Grays, when soot and ash choked the sky and the surface remained unlivable. Headman Vallard Thanaric the First led his people from those depths during the Mass Emergence, becoming the first leader to guide his clan into the world above. Information regarding him is limited, though he is told to have brokered peace and trade with the dwarves of Veiliss and the Gigamar of the Aldemont mountains.

The Vallard line continued through several headmen and chieftains, each ruling the tribes of the northern Aldemont ridges.

### The Warlock Chiefs

Chieftain Vallard VI was a traditionalist who led his tribes through harsh times and prosperous ones alike. He avoided conflict with the Veist people, fearing their ties to the Gresch and their sorceries. The chieftain harbored a deep terror of magic and ill omens, listening obsessively to the wisewomen and always heeding their auguries with caution. Yet this same man showed cruelty toward anyone physically incapable of contributing to the tribe, viewing weakness as a stain upon his lineage.

His son, Vallard VII, was born with a mangled, undeveloped leg. His father received him with no sympathy, seeing him as a failure. The elder hoped the boy would simply die, or better yet, sire another heir and then perish. Yet the chieftain was not cruel enough to kill the child outright, for the boy had been brought back from the wilds by the wisewomen of the forest mountains.

As a babe, Vallard VII had been abandoned in the highland woods to be eaten by wolves, meant to rid the Vallard lineage of its weak heir. Yet a witch from the wild forests found and returned the child, prophesying greatness in him. The chieftain, fearing superstition and magic, allowed his son to live, though without honor or respect.

The crippled boy coveted the displays of strength, masculinity, and power shown by warriors in the tribe. He could not obtain the physical strength he desired. Despite training as best he could, he often became the victim of beatings despite his chieftain's blood. Then he witnessed something that changed everything. Weak witches, frail in body, struck terror into his bullies' hearts with mere presence.

He sought out the wild witches and their domain, learning magics that could bring dozens of men to their knees. Spending years among them, he eventually mastered their arts. When he finally wielded this magic against his tormentors, the tribe declared him profane and exiled him.

He returned with vengeance, reducing his own tribe to ash. Through sheer force, he united the other tribes under his rule. His next objective turned toward prosperous peoples he eyed with envious gleam. He attacked the great city of Valk after the oracle witches of the wild forests augured its fall. Many refugees of the Valk Kingdom ended up as slaves to these barbarians after the events of the Valk Frontier.

His son, Vallard VIII, proved no better than his father. He continued the campaign from a young age, pillaging the destroyed kingdom of Veistyria and raiding Greschian territories. Unlike his crippled father, he waded into battle himself, suffering no physical ailments. Yet he met his end during the great exodus, when the Veist and Gresch people fled toward Eldvatr. King Margric Artas Ordos faced him in battle and struck him down, leaving behind a young heir without his father's guidance.

### The Gentle Chieftain

Unlike his predecessors, Chieftain Vallard IX rejected his forefathers' actions against the refugees from Valk. Instead, he freed them and integrated them into his own tribe. As a youth without a father, he had grown attached to many servants within the tribe and decided it was not right for them to remain shackled and abused.

He proved calmer in temperament than his Warlock Chief father and grandfather. He avoided the magic of the wisewomen, even exiling many of the more brash and ill-tempered practitioners. Though he faced constant strife and infighting due to his peaceful resolutions and measured responses among a people who had known only raiding and pillaging for two generations, he managed to hold his position. His gigantic stature for a human granted him authority in the eyes of his warring people rather than painting him as a coward.

Yet some of his decisions caught up with him. The exile of the wild witches of the forests proved costly. They augured his death and cast a curse upon him. He died young, barely reaching forty years of age.

He left behind an heir, a son he named Siegrick, abandoning the Vallard name entirely. He believed his father and grandfather had soiled it beyond redemption. The chieftain taught young Siegrick the ways of the Valk people and Greschian governance, hoping to shape his son into a ruler of the mind as much as of might.

### The First King

Siegrick followed his father's wishes, learning all that the chieftain hoped to teach him. Though his father was a gentle and loving man despite impressive physical prowess, the boy did not inherit the giant's body. He grew tall but slim, unable to match his father in fighting or physical power. What he lacked in strength, he overcompensated for with words.

Where his father possessed stoic demeanor and spoke few words of wisdom that lent weight to his governance, the son proved fast-talking and charismatic. In his youth, he became a womanizer and scoundrel, abusing his status freely. Yet when his father died and he took the mantle of rule, his personality changed drastically. The loss weighed heavy upon him, marking the end of his youth.

Following his father's teachings and the rare objects left to him, books and tomes from distant lands, he transformed the tribal people of the Aldemont ridge into a kingdom. He named it Lorden, a den of lords and ladies inspired by the fantastical books he had learned from as a boy.

Beyond establishing the monarchy, which he felt honored his father deeply, the new king undertook one notable action. He led his people to more fertile lands below the mountains, dispelling the auguries and superstitions of the wild forest witches that even his father had half-believed. Leaving the mountains had been forbidden by their prophecies, yet he defied them.

This cemented his bloodline as sacred in the eyes of his people.

### The Sagas of the Merry Three

After a peaceful age of fertile lands and an established Kingdom of Lorden, Siegrick Thanaric I died around age sixty, leaving three heirs. The three brothers were Siegrick Thanaric II, a dreamer and scholar who would become Lorden's first wizard; Cattolf the Sly, a cunning courtier; and Sigurd the Mighty, a formidable warrior.

The brothers got along so well that none feared the others would seize the throne. Yet after their father's death, a new era washed over the lands of Norvast. Magbeasts began appearing in greater numbers. Mists and fogs lingered longer through winter, crawling forth from the wild forests into settled lands.

The eldest son took it upon himself to investigate these matters alongside his brothers. Their adventures became the "Sagas of the Merry Three," now spun as children's tales.

According to these sagas, the new king and his brothers alone undertook a journey to discover what plagued their lands. They found the Witches of the Wild hiding deep in the forests, cursing the people of Lorden. The three slew the evil hags and their coven, destroyed their servants by cutting down wyverns and Demiliss from the unknown lands of Wyldric, and even stopped a cult of Greschian sorcerers. They returned to find their kingdom seized by an evil spy of the old King Margric. The accuracy of these events remains highly doubtful.

What history confirms is more modest. The king did discover wild magic in the forests and learned to harness it, allowing him to cast spells. He slew a known coven of witches in the deep forests alongside his brothers and a squadron of knights, though not without casualties and injuries. Most importantly, he began writing one of the first tomes that would later form the foundation of the Arcanist arts.

### The Shadow of Greatness

Bernrend I was born the second son to King Siegrick II. After his older brother died from illness early in life, he inherited a throne he never expected. Unlike his father, he held no love for the arcane or magical arts, nor did he pursue much learning. The old king, consumed by grief over his eldest son's death, hardly paid attention to the second son or cultivated curiosity within him.

He was born into the shadow of a legendary father, now warped by sorrow. Throughout his entire rule, he felt he lived under this shadow. Many times he launched foolish campaigns attempting to differentiate himself from his lineage. He spent most of his reign as a drunkard and joy-seeker, chasing anything that might make him feel worthy of the throne he never wanted.

### The Giant Slayer

Bernrend II proved a mighty warrior-king, unlike his father. He learned the history of his forefathers and led his people toward prosperity. Among his greatest achievements was the campaign against the wild giants dwelling in the forbidden Elstreach woods. These giants had long wreaked havoc across the kingdom, causing countless deaths throughout the region.

After a bloody war pitting a dozen giants against an army of men, the campaign succeeded in driving the giants from the lands of Norvast, or at least most of them. The most legendary moment came when the king faced one of the giants in single combat, physically overpowering the creature for a crucial moment and securing victory. Whether this tale is inflated or proof of inherited wild magic within him remains debated.

These clashes with the giants caused the Auxiliary Brigade to combat monsters of Lorden to first arise.

Later in his reign, he made contact with the Verdigris people, who had originally been part of the Kingdom of Tannen. He conquered them swiftly, driving out most non-humans or forcing them into slavery. Upon their ruins, he established a harbor settlement. The ancient city of Verdigris had been built millennia ago and still stood, filled with passages from ages past. The king built his new harbor atop these weathered foundations, occasionally weathering skirmishes with the Ravanian Empire to the south.

### War and Peace

Bernrend III carried his father's dislike of magic and non-humans, causing war to erupt with the Gilded Isles and the Wyldric Theocracy while also suffering numerous raids from the Silfrkell isles. This brought much turmoil to Lorden. After a harsh war in which the kingdom was crushed under the draconic might of the Demiliss of Wyldric, a treaty of non-aggression was reached. Lorden promised to cease any raids or attacks on their solitude isles.

This treaty was only achieved through the Kingdom of Waltzenoir stepping in to mediate, a kingdom of crystals and glass, apparently established originally by the Ice Witches of Aldemont. Though reclusive, they carried certain weight within magical circles such as Wyldric and Tannen.

Most of the king's rule after the treaty involved dealing with attacks from the southern border by the Ravanian Empire.

Bernrend IV's reign was one of commerce and peace-brokering. He established trade with Wyldric, Silfrkell's silver mines, Tannen, and Waltzenoir. He even attempted to forge connections with the legendary settlement of Grimgon, though with limited success.

He was more warrior and trader than anything else. Most of his warring involved skirmishing with the Ravanian Empire along the border. He once clashed directly with Kaiser Margric himself, losing an eye in the process. His reign also saw the official connection established between the faith and the Auxiliary Brigade.

### The Current King

According to recorded history, Alaric of Thanaric had three siblings. Two were older: Bernrend V, heir to the crown, and Dagan, the middle child plagued with wild magic yet wielding immense talent for warfare and combat.

As the third son of royal blood, Alaric was sent to become a clergyman. Expectations of stature and land ownership still applied to him, yet King Bernrend IV wished to avoid conflicts between his sons over dividing the kingdom. Assigning Alaric to the clergy provided the royal family with a high position within the church while granting Alaric education, honor, and comfortable living.

Alaric, always a gentle youth, accepted his duties with grace. However, Melasadra killed both his brothers during the War of Aldreach, the valley region now controlled by Aldergard and Mordantis.

Alaric was brought from the clergy back to monarchy in his early twenties to be seated as king. He has proven himself a wise, capable, and powerful ruler.

The Monarch of Lorden is now King Alaric Thanaric I, an old human at the age of eighty-one, an immensely old age for the average span of humans within Norvast, Weinost, and Eldvatr. Currently, Alaric I has two sons: Alaric II and Ruvonig.
