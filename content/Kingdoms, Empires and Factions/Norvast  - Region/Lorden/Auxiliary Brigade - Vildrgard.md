The name Yrtgard predates the Brigade's formal recognition by the crown. During the reign of Bernrend III, Lorden's raids and harassment of Wyldric provoked a devastating response. The Demiliss of Wyldric descended upon Lorden with draconic fury, and the kingdom found itself outmatched. Desperate defense fell to whatever warriors could be mustered, including the veterans of Bernrend II's giant campaigns who still remembered how to fight things larger and more terrible than men. These hunters and soldiers formed hasty warbands to intercept Wyldric's beasts before they reached settlements. They called themselves Yrtgard, "wyrm-guard," for the serpentine and draconic creatures they faced. The name stuck even after Waltzenoir brokered peace and the Demiliss withdrew. When the Brigade was later formalized under royal authority, common folk had already begun calling them Vildrgard, a broader term for those who guarded against the wilds. The older veterans kept Yrtgard among themselves, a reminder of what they had survived and what the Brigade was first forged to fight.

The Brigade formed out of necessity during Bernrend II's war against the giants of Elstreach. After the campaign ended, the veterans who had learned to fight such creatures found their skills still needed. Monsters did not stop emerging from the wilds simply because the giants were driven out. These veterans became the first of the Auxiliary Brigade, operating under loose royal authority to handle threats that ordinary soldiers could not.

Malten and the Auxiliary Brigade share history, purpose, and personnel. Many Brigade members trained in Malten or worked there before returning to Lorden. Some split their time between both. The Brigade operates within the eastern wall stretch by agreement, and Malten's research library is open to Brigade members seeking knowledge on specific creatures. In turn, the Brigade shares information about monster movements within Lorden's borders. Some say the Brigade is simply Malten's arm inside Lorden's kingdom, though members reject this.

Bernrend IV tied the Brigade to the Myrkvargr Covenant. Grey Shepherds sometimes travel with hunting parties, blessing weapons and performing rites over slain creatures. Some monsters are believed to be corruptions of the natural order, and the faith provides rituals to ensure they stay dead. Not all Brigade members are devout, but most respect the Shepherds' role. Wisewomen and Cunningfolk also assist the Brigade in remote areas, though this cooperation remains informal and sometimes tense given the history of witch hunts.

The Brigade receives a modest stipend from the crown, barely enough to maintain a handful of gathering halls across the kingdom. Real income comes from bounties posted by local lords, towns, and merchants, as well as salvage rights over slain creatures. Monster parts sell well, particularly in Malten. Alchemists, Arcanists, and craftsmen pay good coin for teeth, hides, organs, and bones. 

Most folk view the Brigade with a mix of gratitude and wariness. They are the ones who come when something crawls out of the woods and starts killing livestock or worse. They are also strange, scarred people who speak casually about horrors and keep odd trophies. Useful, certainly. But usually not the sort you invite to a ceremony.

The Myrk Order considers them useful but undisciplined. The Astralegis sees them as a dumping ground for Arcanists too reckless or unconventional for proper service. The nobility tolerates them because someone has to deal with these things.

The Astralegis maintains a working relationship with the Brigade out of practical necessity. Monster parts are essential components for many rituals, potions, and enchantments. Magbeast blood, organs, bones, and stranger things fetch high prices among Arcanists who lack the skills or inclination to hunt such creatures themselves. Some Arcanists post specific requests through Brigade contacts, paying well for particular specimens delivered intact. Others join hunts personally when they need components too delicate to survive transit or too dangerous to entrust to non-practitioners. Brigade gatherings halls keep ledgers of standing orders from the organization, and certain Field-Arcanists rotate through Brigade service as a reliable way to stock their workshops.

### Guildmansions

The Vildrgard maintains a handful of Guildmansions in Lorden's larger settlements. The oldest and largest sits in Thangard, a weathered stone building near the lower districts that serves as the organization's unofficial headquarters. Here the Huntmaster keeps residence, contracts are posted and recorded, and veteran hunters gather between jobs to drink, trade information, and barter salvage. Smaller Guildmansions exist in Verdigris, Morgenster, and a few other towns where monster activity warrants permanent presence. These are rarely grand, usually repurposed merchant halls or old manor houses granted by local lords in exchange for the Brigade's services. Each Guildmansion keeps its own ledger of local contracts, maintains a small armory, and offers bunks for traveling members. The quality varies wildly depending on local funding and how recently something nasty crawled out of the nearby wilds.

### Structure

Unlike the Myrk Order with its chapter houses and formal hierarchy, the Brigade operates more like a loose fellowship. Members are recognized by the crown but largely manage themselves. They take contracts, collect bounties, and keep what they salvage. The crown provides legitimacy and occasional funding when threats grow severe enough to warrant royal attention. Otherwise, they scrape by.

**Huntmaster** - Runs the Vildrgard. Handles crown relations and Malten coordination. Settles disputes over contracts and salvage.

Vildrwarden - Takes the hardest contracts, leads parties, trains others. Proven, helps manage and run a Vildrgard brigade guildhouse.

**Vildrhunter** - Proven hunter. Works alone or joins parties. Posts and takes contracts. 

**Novice** - New, Unproven. Joins others until they earn standing, they don't earn part of the contracts rewards.
