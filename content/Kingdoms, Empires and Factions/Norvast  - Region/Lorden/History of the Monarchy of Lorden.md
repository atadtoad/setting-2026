## History of the Monarchy

Calling king Vallard a king was stretch even for the scholars of the time, he was more a chief of a clan in the far mountains of the Aldemont Veiliss in ancient times, the information regarding Vallard Thanaric the first is very limited, however, is told he was the first leader to take out the people of now Norvast outside the deep mountain caverns in the age of grays. 

he was known to broker trade and peace with the dwarves of Veiliss and Gigamar of the Aldemont mountains.

##### Chieftain Vallard Thanaric VI (6th)
A traditionalist chieftain, Vallard VI led the tribes of the northern Aldemont ridges through harsh times and prosperous ones alike. He avoided conflict with the Veist people, fearing their ties to the Gresch and their sorceries.

The chieftain harbored a deep terror of magic and ill omens. He listened obsessively to the wisewomen, fearing their auguries and always heeding caution. Yet this same man showed cruelty toward anyone physically incapable of contributing to the tribe, viewing weakness as a stain upon his lineage.
##### Chieftain Vallard Thanaric VII (7th)
Vallard VII, a chieftain born with a mangled, undeveloped leg, coveted the displays of strength, masculinity, and power shown by warriors in the tribe. Under his father, he received no sympathy and was seen as a failure. The elder hoped the boy would simply die, or better yet, bear another heir and then perish. Yet his father was not cruel enough to kill the child outright, as the boy had been brought back from the wilds by the wisewomen of the forest mountains.

As a babe, he had been abandoned in the woods to be eaten by highland wolves, meant to rid the Vallard lineage of its weak heir. Yet against his father's wishes, a witch from the wild forests found and returned the child. This wisewoman prophesied greatness in him. The chieftain, fearing superstition and magic, allowed his son to live, though without honor or respect.

The boy could not obtain the physical strength and power he coveted in men. Despite training as best he could, he often became the victim of beatings despite his chieftain's blood. Then he witnessed something that changed everything. Weak witches, frail in body, struck terror into his bullies' hearts with mere presence.

He sought out the wild witches and their domain, learning magics that could bring dozens of men to their knees. Spending years among them, he eventually mastered their arts. When he finally wielded this magic against his tormentors, the tribe declared him profane and exiled him.

He returned with vengeance, reducing his own tribe to ash. Through sheer force, he united the other tribes under his rule. His next objective turned toward prosperous peoples he eyed with envious gleam. He attacked the great city of Valk after the oracle witches of the wild forests augured its fall.

Many refugees of the Valk Kingdom ended up as slaves to these barbarians after the events of the Valk Frontier.
##### Chieftain Vallard Thanaric VIII (8th)
The son of the Warlock Chief of the Aldemont mountain ridge barbarians proved no better than his father. He continued the campaign from a young age, pillaging the destroyed kingdom of Veistyria and raiding Greschian territories. Unlike his crippled father, he waded into battle himself, suffering no physical ailments.

Yet he met his end during the great exodus, when the Veist and Gresch people fled toward Eldvatr. King Margric Artas Ordos faced him in battle and struck him down. The warlock chief fell, leaving behind a young heir without his father's guidance.
##### Chieftain Vallard Thanaric IX (9th)
Unlike his predecessors, Chieftain Vallard IX rejected his forefathers' actions against the refugees from Valk. Instead, he freed them and integrated them into his own tribe. As a youth without a father, he had grown attached to many servants within the tribe and decided it was not right for them to remain shackled and abused.

He proved calmer in temperament than his Warlock Chief father and grandfather. He avoided the magic of the wisewomen, even exiling many of the more brash and ill-tempered practitioners. Though he faced constant strife and infighting due to his peaceful resolutions and measured responses among a people who had known only raiding and pillaging for two generations, he managed to hold his position. His gigantic stature for a human granted him authority in the eyes of his warring people rather than painting him as a coward.

Yet some of his decisions caught up with him. The exile of the wild witches of the forests proved costly. They augured his death and cast a curse upon him. He died young, barely reaching forty years of age.

He left behind an heir, a son he named Siegrick, abandoning the Vallard name entirely. He believed his father and grandfather had soiled it beyond redemption. The chieftain taught young Siegrick the ways of the Valk people and Greschian governance, hoping to shape his son into a ruler of the mind as much as of might.
##### King Siegrick Thanaric I (1st)
Siegrick followed his father's wishes, learning all that the chieftain hoped to teach him. Though his father was a gentle and loving man despite impressive physical prowess, the boy did not inherit the giant's body. He grew tall but slim, unable to match his father in fighting or physical power. What he lacked in strength, he overcompensated for with words.

Where his father possessed stoic demeanor and spoke few words of wisdom that lent weight to his governance, the son proved fast-talking and charismatic. In his youth, he became a womanizer and scoundrel, abusing his status freely. Yet when his father died and he took the mantle of rule, his personality changed drastically. The loss weighed heavy upon him, marking the end of his youth.

Following his father's teachings and the rare objects left to him, books and tomes from distant lands, he transformed the tribal people of the Aldemont ridge into a kingdom. He named it Lorden, a den of lords and ladies inspired by the fantastical books he had learned from as a boy.

Beyond establishing the monarchy, which he felt honored his father deeply, the new king undertook one notable action. He led his people to more fertile lands below the mountains, dispelling the auguries and superstitions of the wild forest witches that even his father had half-believed. Leaving the mountains had been forbidden by their prophecies, yet he defied them.

This cemented his bloodline as sacred in the eyes of his people.
##### King Siegrick Thanaric II (2nd)
After a peaceful age of fertile lands and an established Kingdom of Lorden, Siegrick Thanaric I died around age sixty, leaving three heirs. The three brothers were Siegrick Thanaric II, a dreamer and scholar who would become Lorden's first wizard; Cattolf the Sly, a cunning courtier; and Sigurd the Mighty, a formidable warrior.

The brothers got along so well that none feared the others would seize the throne. Yet after their father's death, a new era washed over the lands of Norvast. Magbeasts began appearing in greater numbers. Mists and fogs lingered longer through winter, crawling forth from the wild forests into settled lands.

The eldest son took it upon himself to investigate these matters alongside his brothers. Their adventures became the "Sagas of the Merry Three," now spun as children's tales.

According to these sagas, the new king and his brothers alone undertook a journey to discover what plagued their lands. They found the Witches of the Wild hiding deep in the forests, cursing the people of Lorden. The three slew the evil hags and their coven, destroyed their servants by cutting down wyverns and Demiliss from the unknown lands of Wyldric, and even stopped a cult of Greschian sorcerers. They returned to find their kingdom seized by an evil spy of the old King Margric. The accuracy of these events remains highly doubtful.

What history confirms is more modest. The king did discover wild magic in the forests and learned to harness it, allowing him to cast spells. He slew a known coven of witches in the deep forests alongside his brothers and a squadron of knights, though not without casualties and injuries. Most importantly, he began writing one of the first tomes that would later form the foundation of the Arcanist arts.

##### King Bernrend Thanaric I (1st)
Bernrend I was born the second son to King Siegrick II. After his older brother died from illness early in life, he inherited a throne he never expected. Unlike his father, he held no love for the arcane or magical arts, nor did he pursue much learning. The old king, consumed by grief over his eldest son's death, hardly paid attention to the second son or cultivated curiosity within him.

He was born into the shadow of a legendary father, now warped by sorrow. Throughout his entire rule, he felt he lived under this shadow. Many times he launched foolish campaigns attempting to differentiate himself from his lineage. He spent most of his reign as a drunkard and joy-seeker, chasing anything that might make him feel worthy of the throne he never wanted.
##### King Bernrend Thanaric II (2nd)
Bernrend II proved a mighty warrior-king, unlike his father. He learned the history of his forefathers and led his people toward prosperity. Among his greatest achievements was the campaign against the wild giants dwelling in the forbidden Elstreach woods. These giants had long wreaked havoc across the kingdom, causing countless deaths throughout the region.

After a bloody war pitting a dozen giants against an army of men, the campaign succeeded in driving the giants from the lands of Norvast, or at least most of them. The most legendary moment came when the king faced one of the giants in single combat, physically overpowering the creature for a crucial moment and securing victory. Whether this tale is inflated or proof of inherited wild magic within him remains debated.

These clashes with the giants caused the auxiliary brigade to combat monsters of Lorden to first arise.

Later in his reign, he made contact with the Verdigris people, who had originally been part of the Kingdom of Tannen. He conquered them swiftly, driving out most non-humans or forcing them into slavery. Upon their ruins, he established a harbor settlement. The ancient city of Verdigris had been built millennia ago and still stood, filled with passages from ages past. The king built his new harbor atop these weathered foundations, occasionally weathering skirmishes with the Ravanian Empire to the south.
##### King Bernrend Thanaric III (3rd)
Bernrend III carried his father's dislike of magic and non-humans, causing war to erupt with the Gilded Isles and the Wyldric Theocracy while also suffering numerous raids from the Silfrkell isles. This brought much turmoil to Lorden. After a harsh war in which the kingdom was crushed under the draconic might of the Demiliss of Wyldric, a treaty of non-aggression was reached. Lorden promised to cease any raids or attacks on their solitude isles.

This treaty was only achieved through the Kingdom of Waltzenoir stepping in to mediate, a kingdom of crystals and glass, apparently established originally by the Ice Witches of Aldemont. Though reclusive, they carried certain weight within magical circles such as Wyldric and Tannen.

Most of the king's rule after the treaty involved dealing with attacks from the southern border by the Ravanian Empire.
##### King Bernrend Thanaric IV (4th)
Bernrend IV's reign was one of commerce and peace-brokering. He established trade with Wyldric, Silfrkell's silver mines, Tannen, and Walzenoir. He even attempted to forge connections with the legendary settlement of Grimgon, though with limited success.

He was more warrior and trader than anything else. Most of his warring involved skirmishing with the Ravanian Empire along the border. He once clashed directly with Kaiser Margric himself, losing an eye in the process. His reign also saw the official connection established between the faith and the auxiliary brigade.
##### The Current King Alaric Thanaric the First
According to recorded history, Alaric of Thanaric had three siblings. Two were older: Bernrend V, heir to the crown, and Dagan, the middle child plagued with wild magic yet wielding immense talent for warfare and combat.

As the third son of royal blood, Alaric was sent to become a clergyman. Expectations of stature and land ownership still applied to him, yet King Bernrend IV wished to avoid conflicts between his sons over dividing the kingdom. Assigning Alaric to the clergy provided the royal family with a high position within the church while granting Alaric education, honor, and comfortable living.

Alaric, always a gentle youth, accepted his duties with grace. However, Melasadra killed both his brothers during the War of Aldreach, the valley region now controlled by Aldergard and Mordantis.

Alaric was brought from the clergy back to monarchy in his early twenties to be seated as king. He has proven himself a wise, capable, and powerful ruler.

Currently, Alaric I has two sons: Alaric II and Ruvonig.
