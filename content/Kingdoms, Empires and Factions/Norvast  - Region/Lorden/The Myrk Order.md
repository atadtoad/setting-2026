## The Myrk Order

The Myrk Order is an order of royal knights of the Kingdom of Lorden and the martial arm of the Myrkvargr faith. Unlike the broader faithful who worship at shrines and leave offerings, the knights of this order have dedicated their swords to the Black Wolf. They are strongly symbolized by engravings depicting the Black Wolf of Lorden, whom they believe grants them strength and protection. They live by a set of values that they are required to uphold, even though in reality these values are sometimes broken.

### Myrk Knights and Common Knights

A common knight in Lorden serves his liege lord. His duties are to that lord's lands, that lord's wars, that lord's interests. He may worship any faith or none. His oaths are feudal in nature, and when his lord releases him or dies, those oaths may pass to another or end entirely. A common knight holds the right to levy soldiers from his lord's domain, calling upon peasants and men-at-arms as his station permits. Most importantly, a common knight typically holds land, whether inherited or granted through service.

A Myrk Knight is different in every respect. The majority of Myrk Knights are errant knights, men of noble blood who possess no lands of their own. Where a landed knight draws wealth and authority from his holdings, an errant knight has only his sword, his name, and his oaths. For such men, the Myrk Order offers purpose, coin, and the hope of advancement. Membership in the Order is a coveted title, and for errant knights, gaining entry often leads to land being granted soon after as reward for faithful service.

A Myrk Knight is first and foremost a believer in the Myrkvargr faith who has dedicated his sword to the Black Wolf. His service is not to a local lord but directly to the Crown of Lorden, for the Myrkvargr faith holds that the Black Wolf chose the Thanaric bloodline to rule. A Myrk Knight answers to the King and to the priesthood of the Myrkvargr faith. He guards shrines of the Black Wolf, hunts those who defile sacred sites, escorts Grey Shepherds through dangerous lands, and stands ready to answer the Greytongue's summons when crisis demands it.

Unlike common knights, Myrk Knights hold no direct authority to levy folk into their service. Their domain is the Crown, not the land. Most hold no land from which to draw soldiers regardless. However, when need arises, a Myrk Knight may request levies through a local lord. Such arrangements require the lord's agreement, typically negotiated through compensation or favor. Many lords agree readily, for the honor of contributing soldiers to the Myrk Order reflects well upon a house. The Order is known to reward generously those who support its campaigns, whether through coin, royal favor, or simply the prestige of association. A lord whose men fight alongside Myrk Knights earns recognition that a common levy never provides.

Where common knights may be released from service, Myrk Knights serve until death. Where common knights fight for land and title, Myrk Knights fight for the Wolf and the throne it has blessed. They are the sword of the Myrkvargr faith, templars of the Black Wolf, intended from their first oath to one day join the inner Covenant and bind themselves fully to the Wolf itself. Those who prove worthy advance to Lord Knight, undergoing the Covenant's binding ritual and receiving the Wolf's Gift that sets them apart from ordinary men.

One can be a knight in Lorden and belong to a noble house without being part of the Myrk Order. Landed knights with holdings and obligations to their liege lords rarely join, as the Order demands service to Crown above all else. The Myrk Order draws instead from those with less to lose and more to gain: second sons, dispossessed nobility, and errant knights seeking a path to honor and land through faith and sword.

The Myrk Order is far from small, consisting of thousands of soldiers who all report directly to the monarchy. These knights maintain chapter houses near each border of nearby factions or the wilds, commanded by Lord Knights who have undergone the Covenant's binding.

Additionally, these knights undergo rigorous training even after their acceptance into the order. Achieving a higher rank grants them greater honor and financial standing, as well as access to rare resources, magical writings, esoteric potions, and the backing of the monarchy in many endeavors. As such, many of these knights are also scholars and mages who have studied and trained in order to gain access to these resources. Some are members of both the Astralegis and the Myrk Order, along with the opportunity to gain audience with the King.

They are also, at times, sent to assist the Auxiliary Brigade with threats posed by monsters, especially those who have joined the inner Covenant and bear the Wolf's Gift of inhuman strength and speed.

### Order Ranks

**Grand Knight:** Commands the entire order and sits on the King's Council. Required to join the Myrkvargr Covenant, bearing the Wolf's binding and its gifts.

**Lord Knight:** Leads individual chapter houses. Required to join the Myrkvargr Covenant. Those who reach this rank have proven their devotion and undergo the binding ritual, gaining the physical prowess and extended youth that marks all Covenant members.

**Knight:** Sworn knights following orders given by the order's leadership. Not yet of the Covenant, though most aspire to eventually undergo the binding.

**Swordie:** Apprentices training under knights.

### Training and Trials

Upon introduction to the Myrk Order through the recommendation of another Myrk Knight, the Swordie, usually a teenager or slightly older, is placed under the household of a Lord Knight. There, they are enrolled in the practice of swordsmanship and combat under rigorous training. They study the history, laws, and Sagas of Lorden, endure harsh winters, and undergo extreme survival training together, supporting one another to survive, though some still perish.

Beyond martial training, Swordies are instructed in the Myrkvargr faith. They learn the prayers and rites of the Black Wolf, attend services at shrines, and come to understand the beliefs they will one day swear to uphold. By the time they become full knights, they are as much faithful of the Wolf as they are warriors of the Crown.

After serving one winter within the Frontier Vanguard of the army of Lorden and another with the Auxiliary Brigade, a candidate may officially join the Myrk Order following evaluation by a Lord Knight of the Order.

Once accepted as a knight of the Myrk Order, the newly formed knight must forge their own arms and sword with the assistance of the smiths of Lorden, using a unique metal. From it, they craft the Myrsword.
### Myrsword

A Myrsword is a personalized sword created through one's own skill using Myrlith ore, which was given to the line of Thanaric ages past by the dwarves of Veiliss. This ore is extremely limited, and at times new swords are forged by melting down the metal of older ones. The creator of a Myrsword always knows the location of the blade they have made, as forging the sword attunes them to its magical metal.

### Values

King above lord, knights of the order above self. Defend those who cannot defend themselves. Proper burial rites for all knights. Follow orders, even ruthless dishonorable commands. Show no mercy to oath-breakers, even former crestfallen knights.

### Why Some Errant Knights Refuse the Myrk Order

**Service Until Death** A common knight may be released from service when his obligations are fulfilled. A Myrk Knight serves until he dies. For an errant knight who hopes to one day settle, marry, and live quietly on whatever land he earns, the Order offers no such future. The oath is permanent. Some men want a path to peace, not a lifetime of war.

**The Covenant's Shadow** Every Myrk Knight knows that advancement means joining the Myrkvargr Covenant. The binding grants strength, speed, and prolonged youth, but no one speaks of what is given in return. Whispers circulate among errant knights: that the Wolf claims something from those it blesses, that Covenant members age all at once when death finally comes, that their souls belong to the Black Wolf rather than whatever afterlife others expect. Some errant knights look at what the Covenant offers and see a bargain too close to what warlocks make with their patrons. 

**Crown Above Lord** The Myrk Order demands that its knights place the King above any lord. For an errant knight who hopes to eventually swear to a specific noble house, perhaps one that showed his family kindness or one whose daughter he hopes to marry, this oath creates conflict. Joining the Order means abandoning any hope of serving a chosen lord. Some errant knights have loyalties they are unwilling to forsake, even for land and coin.

**Faith Requirements** The Myrk Order is the martial arm of the Myrkvargr faith. Its knights must believe in the Black Wolf, attend shrines, learn prayers, and accept that the Wolf chose the Thanaric bloodline to rule. Not all errant knights share this belief. Some follow the broader Alderdyr faith and honor all Alderbeasts equally, viewing the Myrkvargr claim of the Black Wolf's supremacy as arrogance. Others hold to different faiths entirely, or to none. The Order will not accept a man who cannot swear to the Wolf with conviction.

**Obedience Without Question** The Order's values demand that knights follow orders, even ruthless dishonorable commands. For an errant knight who prizes his own judgment, who left his previous lord precisely because he refused to carry out shameful acts, this requirement is unacceptable. The Myrk Order offers no room for conscience. A knight does as he is told, or he is an oath-breaker, and the Order shows no mercy to oath-breakers.

**The Trials Kill** Not all who enter the Myrk Order survive training. Swordies perish during harsh winters and extreme survival trials. An errant knight past his youth, already skilled with a blade, might see little reason to risk death in training alongside boys half his age. Other paths to land exist, slower perhaps, but less likely to leave a man frozen in a ditch.
