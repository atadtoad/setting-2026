## Dwarves

The dwarves are known as the Myr in Alderil. To themselves, they are the Ebehume.

### Origin and the Shaping

Dwarves reproduce biologically. However, their deep and long-lost homeland possessed gravity so intense that their bodies adapted to depend upon it. Dwarven newborns require this crushing pressure to survive. Without it, they die.

To solve this, dwarves developed the practice of Shaping. A Shaping is an incubator crafted in the form of a small statue, made from metal, stone, or earth. The parent dwarves carve runes and intricate designs across its surface, imbuing it with intent and purpose. These runes mimic the intense gravity of the lost homeland, creating a crucible of pressure within.

A newborn dwarf is placed inside and sealed away. Within the Shaping, the infant enters a state of stillness, a hibernation between life and stone that allows them to endure conditions they otherwise could not survive. The crushing pressure of the runes sustains them as they "bake," slowly developing the density and resilience needed to exist in the lesser gravity of the world above. Over time, as the runes interact with the developing child, the statue cracks, and a dwarven toddler emerges, strong enough to survive outside.

This process can take anywhere from a single month to a full decade, depending on the intricacy of the runes, the materials used, and the shape of the incubator.

Not all Shapings crack. Those that remain sealed are called Serene Statues. Whether the child within has died, sleeps eternally, or simply requires more time, none can say. Serene Statues are kept in mourning halls, tended by caretakers who visit them across centuries in the hope that one day they might still wake.

### The Stillness

Adult dwarves can also enter the state of stillness, They must craft a statue large enough to contain themselves, carve the proper runes, and seal themselves within. This is a ritual undertaken only in dire circumstances: to survive mortal wounds, to heal from injuries that would otherwise claim their lives, or to endure when the world itself becomes unlivable.

In this stillness, a dwarf becomes indistinguishable from stone. Their body hardens. Their vital functions cease. Time passes without touching them. However, the risk is real: a dwarf who enters this state may never wake, becoming a Serene Statue themselves. The length of time one can safely remain in hibernation is unknown, even to the dwarves.

### First Contact with Humanity

When humans first descended into the underground during the Age of Grays, they encountered dwarven settlements filled with Shapings in various stages. They also found larger statues, adult-sized, arranged in halls and chambers. Humans assumed them all to be carvings.

According to dwarven accounts, their people had been trapped in this stillness because the world had become one they could not survive in. When the Lightfall occurred, they reawoke.

### Veiliss

Veiliss is a region of massive mountains and frozen wastes, home to vast underground realms beneath the Aldemont range. These halls are older than any human kingdom. When exactly they were carved, none can say.

Veiliss is not a single kingdom but many. Multiple dwarven factions and inner kingdoms occupy the mountains and the depths below. Each maintains its own traditions, its own rulers, its own interpretation of dwarven custom.

Some of these realms extend deeper than any human has traveled. The dwarves speak of older halls below, passages that descend into darkness even they do not enter.

### Lifespan and Aging

Dwarves grey and grow older, but they do not grow weaker. They grow slower. With age, a dwarf becomes denser and heavier. An old dwarf weighs significantly more than a young one of the same size.

In their final years, movement becomes laborious. Eventually, they can no longer rise. Death comes when they become too dense to sustain their own organs. When that moment arrives, it happens suddenly, as if by spell. The dwarf turns to statue.

These ancestor-statues are among the finest materials for crafting a Shaping. The stone that forms from a dwarf's final transformation is known to men as Myrlith. To the dwarves themselves, it is Ebenlith.
Their age flatulates from 300 to 600 years.

### Physical Traits

Dwarves possess a natural resistance to poison and disease. Their palates reflect this resilience. Dwarves favor extremely spicy food, overly sweet confections, and other strong flavors that would overwhelm a human tongue.

### Temperament and the Book of Tallies

Dwarves are slow to anger. Insults that would drive a human to violence are met with silence, noted but not acted upon. This patience should not be mistaken for forgiveness. A dwarf remembers.

Many dwarves carry a Book of Tallies, a personal ledger in which they record the good and bad deeds done to them and by whom. Over decades and centuries, these ledgers grow. Older dwarves maintain massive tomes in their homes, too heavy to carry, which they update from smaller ledgers they keep on their person.

When a dwarf finally acts upon a grudge, it is with the weight of every accumulated slight behind it. Dwarven wars are measured in decades, not years.

### Society

Social standing among dwarves is influenced by the High-Book of Honor. A dwarf known for accumulating debts of kindness owed to them holds influence. A dwarf whose ledger shows they owe many debts to others is pressured to settle accounts. Reputation is literally written down by dwarves known as Deed-Scribes.

Dwarven society is divided by craft. Smiths, Shapers, Miners, Runescarvers, and others each form distinct social groups with their own traditions and internal hierarchies. A dwarf is born into no caste but chooses one upon reaching maturity. Changing castes later in life is possible but viewed as instability.

### Religion and Beliefs

Dwarves honor their ancestors, particularly those whose forms still rest in the halls where they entered their final stillness. These ancestor-statues are visited, spoken to, and consulted in times of difficulty. Whether the ancestors hear is a matter of faith. Some dwarves believe the stillness is merely sleep, and that in great need, an ancestor might wake once more.

Dwarves have no concept of an afterlife separate from the physical world. When a dwarf dies, their body remains as stone. That is immortality enough. The idea of a soul departing to another realm strikes most dwarves as wasteful and unlikely.

Some dwarves believe that the runes carved into a Shaping do more than protect and nurture the child within. They believe the runes carry memory, that a portion of the carver's knowledge and experience passes into the newborn through the act of inscription. Others dismiss this as superstition.

The dwarves follow several religions that vary in their beliefs. However, all dwarven faiths share an ancient foundation known as the Deepstones. This common core causes their priests and holy places to share certain traits. The holiest dwarven spaces are those where no sound has been made in centuries. Some dwarven priests take vows of silence lasting decades.

They revere the Deepstones, the unknowable source from which the first dwarves emerged in times before memory. The Deep is not a being but a place, or perhaps a state of existence, something below even the lowest halls of the region Veiliss. No dwarf has seen it. All dwarves believe it exists. When a dwarf enters their final stillness, they are said to "return to the Deep."

### Relations with Other Races

Dwarves view humans as chaotic and short-lived, but useful. They trade ore and craft for food and surface goods they do not produce.

The gift of Myrlith ore to the Thanaric line of Lorden was payment for some great service rendered in ages past.

Some dwarves have attempted to craft Shapings using human materials: bone, blood, hair. The results were considered abominations.

### Current Status

The dwarves have grown reclusive since the Age of Grays. Trade continues, but fewer dwarves emerge from the mountains each generation. Some whisper the Shapings are cracking less often. The dwarves may be a dying people, though they measure such things in centuries.

Relations with Lorden remain cordial due to ancient treaties. Relations with other kingdoms such as Tannen, Wyldric, Grimgon, and Nachtgard are minimal or nonexistent.
