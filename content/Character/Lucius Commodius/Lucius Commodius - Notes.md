# Lucius Commodius


## Character Background

Lucius Commodius is thirteen years old, born the second child and only son of **Quintus Commodius Custos**, a soldier of Equestrian rank from an old Veistri military house, and **Fabia Prima Commodia**, born of House Fabia. He has one sister, **Commodia Flavia**, sixteen, currently serving as an Aurian priestess of the Divinor Imperium.

---

### The Family

House Commodius is old without being remarkable. Their Equestrian rank was not bought or inherited through political maneuvering — it was built over generations of military service, one campaign at a time, one name added to a war memorial at a time. They are not a family that produces senators or proconsuls. They produce soldiers. Good ones. That is their pride and their identity and the unspoken expectation placed on every child born under their name.

The household is modest by Equestrian standards. Well maintained, properly appointed, a Divinas shrine burning the correct candles in the correct colors. Fabia Prima Commodia runs it with quiet competence. She is a woman of bearing who came from a respectable House and married into a respectable one and has never given anyone cause to question either choice. She loves her children in the steady, practical way of a woman who understands that love and softness are not always the same thing.

Quintus Commodius Custos is frequently absent. He has always been a man of duty first, and his duties take him away from home on trips the family understands little about beyond that they are necessary and that he will return when he returns. He is not a cold father — when present he is attentive, direct, and genuinely invested in his son — but presence has never been something Lucius could rely on. This journey, the first time Quintus has ever taken Lucius with him, is not a small thing. It may be the most significant gesture his father has ever made toward him.

---

### The Blood

House Commodius carries Veistri blood predominantly, as any proud old military family of their standing would. But somewhere in their lineage, far enough back that no one recorded it cleanly, a Greschian ancestor slipped into the bloodline. It was not discovered until approximately 120 years ago when a young girl of the house was examined and found to carry Aurian potential. That girl was **Julia Antonia Flavia**.

She is still alive.

Aurian priestesses of sufficient devotion live far beyond ordinary human spans, and Julia Antonia Flavia has outlived everyone who ever knew her as young. She has watched the Commodius bloodline for over a century, patient in the particular way that only extreme longevity teaches. She knew before any examiner confirmed it that Commodia Flavia carried the gift. She had been watching both children from birth.

She celebrated Commodia Flavia's revelation with genuine warmth. She has always been warm to her great great granddaughter — attentive, present, generous with her time in the way she is generous with almost nothing else.

To Lucius she is cordial. Nothing less, nothing more. She looked at him, noted what was there, noted what was not, and made her quiet assessment. She does not dislike him. He simply does not interest her.

Lucius has understood this since he was old enough to understand anything. He is not stupid. He knows exactly what her warmth toward his sister means and exactly what its absence toward him means. He has never said this aloud to anyone.

---

### The Examination

When Lucius was tested at nine for Greschian sorcery potential, the examiner found something. Not much. A faint flicker, the ghost of Julia Antonia Flavia's blood showing itself just enough to be noted and then set aside. The examiner moved on. Lucius went home. Somewhere in a Laboratorium ledger his name sits next to a single marginal notation, filed and forgotten by everyone except possibly the people who make it their business to forget nothing.

He was not taken into the Laboratorium. He was not taken into the priesthood. He was handed back to his family with the unspoken verdict that whatever potential lived in him was insufficient to change the course of his life.

His sister was examined the 3 years prior. She did not come home.

---

### The Resentment

Lucius loves his sister. This is true and uncomplicated and sits alongside something else entirely that he has never examined too closely because examining it would require admitting it is there.

Commodia Flavia did not earn her gift any more than he earned its absence. He knows this. The same blood, the same parents, the same house — and the universe distributed its gifts without consultation and handed everything to one of them. She will learn Aurian magic. She will live two centuries. She will stand in proximity to the Emperor himself, inhabit a life of purpose and luxury and meaning that was simply handed to her at age nine by a stranger's nod.

He will fight in the mud. He will prove himself the hard way, campaign by campaign, rank by rank, earning through sweat and discipline what she received as a birthright she never asked for. Julia Antonia Flavia will embrace his sister and be cordial to him and the family will smile and call it all the Kaiser's blessing and Lucius will stand in the room and know exactly what he is looking at.

He carries this quietly. It has not made him bitter in the way that poisons — not yet — but it has made him determined in a way that has an edge to it. He wants to prove something. He is not entirely sure to whom. Possibly to Julia. Possibly to his father. Possibly to himself. Possibly to all three, for different reasons that he hasn't untangled yet.

He is thirteen. He has time to figure it out. Or time to let it curdle. It could go either way.

---

### Now

Lucius Commodius is traveling with his father for the first time. He is about to enter a military school that will prepare him for the life House Commodius has always produced — a soldier's life, earned through discipline and service. He carries his family's name, his father's face, his mother's dark hair, and a faint notation in a forgotten ledger that may or may not ever mean anything.

He does not yet have a cognomen. He hasn't done anything to earn one. That is, in its own way, the whole story waiting to happen.
