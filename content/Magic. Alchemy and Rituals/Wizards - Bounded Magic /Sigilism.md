## Sigilism

Sigilism is one of the few magical traditions open to any humanoid capable of learning it. It requires no binding to the Circle of Gheliach, no innate bloodline potency, and no soul covenant. What it demands instead is a lifetime of study and the willingness to bleed.

The writing of Sigilism derives from Gheli, the old language of Gheliach. Practitioners do not speak or invoke it. They write it, in blood. The language pre-dates every kingdom built atop Gheliach's ruins. It was recovered and reconstructed by scholars of the Ravanian Magis Laboratorium roughly two centuries ago from carvings found in the deepest surviving stonework of those ruins. What they built into teachable practice they called Sigilism.

### The Writing System

A sigil is not a single mark. It is a structured composition in Gheli, with each element carrying meaning and function.

The **inner symbol** names the effect: fire, force, light, bind, drain. It sits enclosed within a circle, sealed.

**Modifier symbols** are secondary circles arranged around the inner symbol, each encircling their own Gheli word. They shape how the inner effect behaves. The way a modifier connects to the inner symbol changes how it contributes:

- A **straight line** between modifier and inner symbol creates a linear, constant flow.
- A **curved line** creates a soft, slow, building contribution.
- Modifiers arranged into a **triangle** around the inner circle direct their energy sharply and quickly inward.
- Arranged into a **square**, they produce a balanced, even distribution.
- Arranged into a further **circle**, they create a cycling, self-sustaining loop useful for sigils intended to maintain themselves over time.

This internal logic is what makes Sigilism simultaneously learnable by any humanoid and one of the most demanding disciplines practiced. Every symbol must be precise. A closed circle improperly drawn, a line meeting at the wrong angle, a modifier placed in a position that implies triangulation when the practitioner intended a cycling loop: any of these produces failure. Failure in a weak sigil means it does not activate. Failure in a complex multilayered inscription can mean something considerably worse.

#### A simple light sigil, for example:

The inner symbol is **heat**, enclosed in its circle. Around it, three modifier symbols are arranged in a circle, creating a self-sustaining loop.

The first modifier is **energy**, connected by a straight line, providing a constant steady input. The second is **inhale**, connected by a curved line, drawing ambient warmth from the surrounding environment slowly and gradually as fuel. The third is **exhale**, connected by a straight line, venting excess heat at a constant steady rate.

The result is a sigil that produces light by sustaining a controlled flame. The inhale draws from the environment rather than burning through the inscription's own blood, extending its life. The exhale prevents heat from accumulating and destroying the sigil from within. Remove the inhale and the sigil consumes itself. Remove the exhale and the trapped heat ruptures it. The curved inhale line ensures the environmental draw is gradual enough not to destabilize the working. The straight exhale keeps the venting consistent and controlled.
### Source and Relation to Greschian Sorcery

Both Sigilism and Greschian Sorcery draw from the same root: Gheliach, and the energy that pools and persists within that ancient place. Greschian sorcery reaches that source through soul-binding to the Bounded Circle and the demon-pool beneath it. The power is enormous and immediate.

Sigilism reaches the same source through written inscription. It is ordered, stable, slower, and significantly weaker than a Greschian working of equivalent intent. It carries no binding cost and demands no covenant. A practitioner who learns Sigilism has not bound themselves to anything, they have simply learned to read and write in a language old enough to draw from Gheliach's power directly.

The red light that fills Valcris at night comes from this: thousands of architectural sigils inscribed into the city's foundations and walls, maintained on rotation by the Laboratorium's Sigilmeisters. The glow is the same red as Greschian fire. The source is the same. Only the channel differs.

### Blood as Medium

When a Sigilist inscribes a sigil in their own blood, the inscription is active. Their potency flows through it, binding their inner self to the Gheliach source and completing the conduit. This is the strongest form Sigilism takes.

Using another Sigilist's blood in the inscription is possible but produces a diminished working. The foreign blood connects to Gheliach's energy alone, without the carver's own inner current reinforcing it. The result is a sigil that functions but never reaches its full potency.

Animal blood, monster blood, or the blood of non-practitioners rarely functions at all. Ravanian blood, carrying the Greschian bloodline inheritance, works as a poor substitute in rare circumstances. The blood of Aurian priestesses, already altered by their gold-binding ritual and connected to Gheliach through bloodline, also occasionally substitutes, though practitioners who have tested this report the results resist control. Neither substitute is used unless necessity forces the matter.

Blood loses potency rapidly once separated from its source. An active sigil inscribed from a fresh cut holds for hours before the magic fades and the marks become nothing more than dried matter on whatever surface they were written.

### Bloodinks

For sigils intended to last, Sigilists use prepared bloodinks: pre-mixed compounds that still require the addition of the carver's own blood to the inkwell at the time of inscription. Without that addition, the ink does not activate.

Bloodinks extend the active lifespan of a sigil at the cost of reduced potency. They are the standard material for utility sigils: heating, lighting, warding, and similar applications where longevity matters more than force.

**Goldblood Inks** are the finest and most expensive grade. A mixture of gold dust and a bound Sigilist's blood, prepared under specific conditions. Gold's function as a stabilizer and amplifier for blood-based magics makes it the natural refinement agent. A goldblood sigil is more stable, longer lasting, and more consistent in output than any other ink formulation, while remaining weaker than a pure fresh-blood inscription. The preparation cost keeps goldblood inks out of common use, reserved for permanent architectural installations and high-priority military applications.

The instrument used to apply bloodink is called a **Carver**: a hollow pen-like implement typically fashioned from Sablesteel with gold adornment, shaped after the profile of a feather. A shared inkwell requires the carver to be touched to the Sigilist's own blood before drawing, so that their potency enters the mixture at the point of writing.

### Medium and Duration

The surface on which a sigil is inscribed affects how long it remains active.

Sablesteel holds longer than any other material, which contributes to its value beyond military applications and is one reason the Empire guards access to it carefully.

Stone holds well. Wood fades faster. Parchment faster still.

Skin is the exception. Fresh-cut living skin, with blood still close to the surface, replenishes the inscription's vitality as long as the subject lives. A sigil written directly on a living body does not expire in hours. It persists. This gives rise to two distinct practices: **Sigilbrand**, the marking of oneself or a willing subject for lasting protection, enhancement, or utility; and **Sigilhex**, the marking of an enemy in warfare to sustain a hostile sigil on their body for as long as they draw breath. The Sigilhex is considered one of the more disturbing tools a war-Sigilist brings to the field.

### Layered Sigils and Scale

Layered sigils, circles inscribed within or atop other circles with their own modifier networks, produce compound effects. Each additional layer costs more blood and draws more from the Sigilist carving it. There is a hard physical limit to how complex a sigil any one practitioner can complete before blood loss or exhaustion ends the work mid-inscription.

An incomplete sigil is an open circle. An open circle releases rather than contains. Sigilists learn early how to abort a composition safely, which marks to close first when the body gives out before the work is done.

Scale changes magnitude. A sigil the size of a palm performs its function at the scale of a palm. The same composition inscribed across the floor of a room performs it with force proportionate to its size. The architectural workings beneath Valcris are enormous inscriptions requiring teams and rotating maintenance, their scale the reason the city glows rather than merely flickers.

### Greschian Sigilists and Vitrumism

Greschian sorcerers who study Sigilism gain one capability pure Sigilists lack. Through Vitrumism, the Greschian art of crystallizing blood, they can set a sigil into a surface before it is meant to activate. The crystallized blood holds the inscription inert, the circuit incomplete and waiting. Decrystallizing it, either deliberately or by a triggering condition, completes the sigil and activates it.

This makes Greschian Sigilists capable of prepared traps, delayed workings, and inscriptions that activate on contact or command rather than immediately upon writing. It is a significant tactical advantage and one reason the Magis Laboratorium actively encourages its Greschian sorcerers to cross-train in at least rudimentary Sigilism.
