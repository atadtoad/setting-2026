## Gravon

Gravon magic is the manipulation of gravity itself. It is considered one of the most primal magics known to exist, predating the Lightfall and perhaps predating humanity's awareness of magic entirely. Unlike most magical traditions which arose during or after the Age of Grays, Gravon magic appears to have always been present, woven into the fabric of the world. The dwarves did not invent it. They merely learned to use what was already there.

### A Hybrid Magic

Gravon magic occupies a unique position among magical classifications. It functions as both Esoteric magic and Bounded Circle magic simultaneously.

Some dwarves are born with innate attunement to gravity, able to manipulate weight and pressure through instinct alone. These natural Gravons require no training to use their abilities at a basic level, though mastery still demands practice. Their magic is wild in the truest sense, tied to bloodline and emerging unpredictably. This places them firmly within the Esoteric tradition.

Yet Gravon magic can also be learned. Dwarven grimoires contain extensive documentation of gravitational manipulation: runes, geometric shapes, formulae, and bounded circles designed to channel the primal force. A dwarf without innate attunement can study these texts, carve the proper symbols, and learn to impose weight upon the world through craft rather than birthright. This places Gravon magic equally within the Bounded Circle tradition.

The two approaches are not separate paths. They intertwine. A natural Gravon who studies the grimoires compounds their innate power with learned technique, achieving far greater mastery than either approach alone would allow. The runes amplify what the blood provides. The instinct guides what the circles contain.

### Innate Attunement

Those born with natural attunement to Gravon magic are almost always dwarven. The connection to their lost homeland, where crushing gravity was necessary for survival, runs deep in their blood. Some scholars believe that all dwarves carry a dormant seed of this attunement, and that natural Gravons are simply those in whom the seed has awakened.

Rarely, other humanoids manifest innate Gravon abilities. These cases are poorly understood. Some suggest distant dwarven ancestry, though the individuals in question often show no other dwarven traits. Others theorize that exposure to intense gravitational forces, whether through proximity to certain monsters or time spent in the deep places of the world, can trigger latent attunement. Whatever the cause, non-dwarven Gravons are exceedingly rare and often struggle to find teachers willing to instruct them.

Those with innate attunement who also study the grimoires experience compounding growth. Each advancement in learned technique amplifies their natural gift. Each deepening of instinct makes the learned techniques more potent. This feedback loop allows attuned Gravons to achieve levels of power that purely learned practitioners cannot match.

### The Grimoires and Circles

Dwarven Gravon grimoires are among the oldest magical texts in existence. Many predate the Lightfall, written in scripts that even modern dwarves struggle to fully interpret. The runes contained within are not merely instructional. They are functional, designed to be carved into stone or metal and activated through specific rituals.

The geometric shapes found in these grimoires follow patterns found nowhere else in magical practice. Circles within circles, triangles folded upon themselves, spirals that seem to pull the eye inward. Scholars who study them report headaches and disorientation after extended examination, as though the shapes themselves exert a subtle pull.

A practitioner who learns Gravon magic through study must first carve a bounded circle according to the grimoire's specifications. This circle serves as an anchor, a fixed point from which gravitational manipulation becomes possible. With practice, the practitioner can eventually work without the physical circle, having internalized its patterns. But the foundation remains: learned Gravon magic traces back to those ancient geometric forms.

### Primal Nature

Gravon magic is considered one of the primal magics, forces that existed before the Aperturing and the Lightfall, before humanity crawled from the underground, perhaps before life itself. Where other magics seem to have entered the world through the Lightfall's breach, gravity simply is. It requires no external source. It draws from no demonic realm or bounded entity. It is a fundamental property of existence, and Gravon magic is merely the art of speaking to it.

This primal nature makes Gravon magic remarkably stable compared to other traditions. Practitioners rarely develop Ma'diya. The magic does not corrupt or warp the mind because it demands nothing in return. It simply responds to those who know how to ask.

Some dwarven theologians believe Gravon magic is connected to the Deep, the unknowable source their people revere. If the Deep is a place of ultimate density and pressure, then Gravon magic may be the Deep's influence reaching upward into the world. This remains speculation, but it carries weight among the faithful.

### Monsters and Colossi

Dwarves are not the only beings that utilize gravitational manipulation. Certain monsters display abilities that can only be described as Gravon magic, though they possess no grimoires and undergo no training.

The Colossi are the most obvious example. Their sheer size should make movement impossible. Bones should shatter. Organs should collapse. Yet they walk, and the ground trembles beneath them. Some scholars believe the Colossi maintain a constant gravitational field that reduces their effective weight, allowing their impossible bodies to function. Others suggest they increase gravity around their limbs selectively, anchoring themselves with each step. Whatever the mechanism, Colossi appear to be naturally attuned to gravitational forces in ways that dwarf even the most powerful dwarven Gravons.

Lesser monsters also display gravitational abilities. Certain deep-dwelling creatures move through stone as though swimming through water, apparently reducing its density around themselves. Flying beasts far too large for their wings have been observed, their mass seemingly lessened. Burrowing horrors create tunnels that should collapse but somehow remain stable, the weight of the earth held at bay.

Whether these creatures possess true Gravon magic or merely some related ability remains debated. What is clear is that gravitational manipulation is not unique to dwarves. It appears wherever sufficient size or depth demands it, as though the world itself teaches this magic to those who need it to survive.

### The Amplification Phenomenon

Scholars both dwarven and human have documented a peculiar interaction between Gravon magic and other forms of spellcraft. When a Gravon increases gravitational pressure around themselves, magic cast within that field grows stronger. The effect is measurable and consistent. A Sigilist's runes burn brighter. An Arcanist's wards hold firmer. Even wild magics seem to intensify.

No one fully understands why this occurs. The prevailing theory among dwarven Runescarvers is that the increased pressure compresses magical energy, concentrating it into smaller space and therefore greater potency. Others suggest that gravity and magic share some fundamental connection, that the same forces which allow Shapings to function are at work here. Whatever the cause, the practical applications are significant. Gravons working alongside other spellcasters can dramatically enhance their effectiveness.

This amplification does not come free. The Gravon must maintain the gravitational field, which requires sustained concentration. The greater the pressure, the greater the strain. Extended amplification leaves Gravons exhausted, and overexertion can render them unable to use their abilities for days.

### Limitations

Gravons cannot affect gravity selectively within their field. If they increase the weight pressing down upon an enemy, they increase it upon themselves as well. Most Gravons train extensively to function under their own pressure, but this remains a fundamental constraint. A Gravon who creates a field intense enough to immobilize an opponent must be strong enough to move within that same field.

For most Gravons, the magic has no effect at range beyond the practitioner's field. The field extends outward from their body, and its strength diminishes rapidly with distance. A typical Gravon cannot crush a distant enemy or lighten a burden they cannot reach.

Additionally, Gravon magic cannot be turned off entirely. Even when not actively manipulating gravity, Gravons exert a subtle pull. Objects near them feel slightly heavier. This passive effect is minor, but it marks them to those who know what to look for. Other Gravons can sense each other by this faint distortion.

### Distant Gravons

Roughly one in twelve Gravons manifests a rarer ability: the power to manipulate gravity at a distance. These Distant Gravons can create points of high or low gravity far from their own bodies, pulling creatures toward a chosen spot or lightening an area to disrupt footing and balance. Where a common Gravon must stand within the crushing weight they create, a Distant Gravon can impose that weight elsewhere while remaining unaffected.

More significantly, areas of lowered gravity seem to nullify or weaken certain magics. Spells cast within these lightened fields lose potency. Runes flicker and fade. Bounded circle effects sputter. This suggests that the relationship between gravity and magical amplification works in both directions: just as increased pressure strengthens magic, decreased pressure weakens it.

Some scholars believe this phenomenon explains why mages grow stronger the deeper they descend into the earth. The pressure of stone and depth may amplify magical potency in the same way a Gravon's field does. If true, the deep places of the world are not merely dangerous due to monsters and darkness, but fundamentally more magical, and Distant Gravons who create low-gravity fields may be inadvertently suppressing this natural amplification.

Distant Gravons appear to sacrifice something for their expanded reach. None have ever manifested Tenciard abilities. Where other Gravons occasionally display both gifts, Distant Gravons seem locked into their singular path. Furthermore, they cannot bind themselves to other magical circles or traditions. The only exceptions are other primal magics, those rare forces that, like gravity, predate the Lightfall and exist as fundamental properties of the world. Whatever grants them range seems to close off other avenues of magical development entirely.
