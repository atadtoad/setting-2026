Every person possesses a certain level of magical potency within them, a measure of raw power that determines their capacity to channel magic. This potency is not fixed. It deepens and expands with age and repeated use of magic, growing stronger the more it is exercised. Unlike physical strength, potency does not decline with age. It continues to grow throughout a mage's entire life, which is both blessing and curse. Some theorize this relentless expansion is the very mechanism that leads to the eventual development of Ma'diya, mage-madness. The destabilization of potency as it grows too large creates fractures in the mind, cracks through which madness seeps. This means that greater potency grants stronger channeling of magic, but also places one at far greater risk of losing oneself to it. Every old mage faces the same question: will their power consume them before their body fails?

Potency runs in bloodlines, but inheritance is unpredictable and cruel in its randomness. Children of high-potency parents are more likely to possess the gift themselves, but it is far from guaranteed. Two powerful mages might produce a child with barely a spark, while a family with no magical history spanning generations might suddenly birth a prodigy. Even siblings from the same parents can possess vastly different levels, the gift distributed without clear logic. This higher likelihood without certainty explains why ancient magical bloodlines obsessively track their genealogies, searching for patterns that exist but refuse to be reliable, trying to predict what remains stubbornly unpredictable.

Yet potency alone does not make a skilled caster. High potency without training is simply raw force waiting to explode, dangerous to the caster and everyone nearby. Wild mages are proof of this. They possess tremendous power with no discipline to control it. Conversely, a mage with modest potency but exceptional training can outperform a more naturally gifted rival through precision, technique, and clever application of what power they do possess. The academies teach this truth early: skill shapes power into something useful. Power without skill is just noise.

Most mages eventually encounter their ceiling, the point beyond which their potency refuses to grow no matter how much they practice or age. Some reach this limit young, forced to accept they will never advance beyond a certain threshold. Others grow for decades before stalling. A rare few seem to have no ceiling at all, their potency expanding without end until Ma'diya claims them or they die from other causes. No one knows what determines where a mage's ceiling lies, though many have theorized it relates to the spark they were born with, or perhaps to choices made early in their training.

Potency can be damaged or reduced, though such wounds are rare and difficult to inflict. Overuse of magic, catastrophic ritual failures, breaking sworn oaths, or surviving magical backlash can scar potency permanently. Some mages never recover, their capacity to channel magic diminished forever. Others lose it entirely, left hollow and unable to touch the power they once wielded. In extreme cases, mages have deliberately crippled their own potency to avoid the onset of Ma'diya, accepting reduction in power as the price for keeping their sanity.

Environmental sensitivity to potency is uncommon but documented. Most mages feel no difference in their power based on location. However, rare individuals report fluctuations when deep underground, in caverns or ancient ruins far below the earth's surface. Their potency seems to intensify in these depths, as though something below resonates with the magic within them. Whether this is a property of the earth itself or of what lies buried beneath it remains a matter of debate among scholars.

Those born with minimal potency face a choice made by others. Depending on how small the spark is, they are either taken for magical development or dismissed entirely. However, potency is not entirely predictable. It can increase abruptly through traumatic events, near-death experiences, or moments of extreme conviction. This phenomenon is what allows certain sorceries to function at all. Oath magic binds conviction to power. Aurian sorceries draw from devotion and faith. Both rely on potency surging in response to the caster's will and circumstances.

There are also dangerous methods to artificially expand one's potency. Binding to magical circles, consuming the flesh of magbeasts and monsters, or undergoing alchemical infusion can force growth beyond natural limits. These procedures are considered highly dangerous, not merely for the physical toll they exact, but for the risk of destabilizing potency entirely. Forced expansion can trigger Ma'diya prematurely, or worse, shatter the caster's capacity for magic altogether, leaving them broken in ways healing cannot repair.
