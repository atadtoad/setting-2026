## Tenciard

Tenciards are beings born with an innate magical constitution that renders their bodies significantly more durable, vital, and resistant to magic than ordinary members of their kind. Unlike Gravon magic, which is considered primal and can be learned through grimoires, Tenciard abilities are purely Esoteric in nature. The magic is always present, always active, woven into the very flesh of those who possess it.

### Origins

Tenciard abilities manifest almost exclusively in dwarven and Gigamar bloodlines. Why these two races share this trait remains unknown. Dwarves and Gigamar possess no common ancestry that scholars have been able to identify. Their origins, histories, and natures differ greatly. Yet both races produce Tenciards, and no satisfactory explanation has ever been offered.

Some theorize that the magic responds to existing resilience, manifesting only in those whose bodies already exceed human limits. Both dwarves and Gigamar are naturally hardier than humans, possessing constitutions that allow them to endure what would kill ordinary folk. Perhaps Tenciard magic is drawn to such bodies, or perhaps it can only take root in flesh already tempered beyond the human norm. This theory explains nothing about the mechanism, only the pattern.

Others suggest that both races unknowingly share exposure to some common source: a place, a substance, a force encountered somewhere in their histories. If such a source exists, it has never been found.

The magic itself offers no answers. It simply is. It takes races already built to endure and pushes them to an extreme.

Rarely, other humanoids manifest Tenciard abilities. These individuals almost always possess distant dwarven or Gigamar ancestry, even if the connection has been forgotten across generations. A human family with no knowledge of non-human blood in their lineage might suddenly produce a child who shrugs off injuries that should prove fatal. Investigation often reveals a dwarven great-great-grandmother or a Gigamar ancestor somewhere in the family tree. The gift seems to sleep in diluted blood, waiting for the right combination to wake it.

The magic requires no activation or conscious effort. A Tenciard does not choose to become durable. They simply are. From birth, their bodies resist damage, recover quickly, and deflect magical influence. The gift can be amplified through focus and training, pushed to greater heights in moments of need, but it never turns off. Even a sleeping Tenciard remains harder to harm than an ordinary person.

### Physical Manifestation

Tenciards possess constitution and vitality far beyond what their apparent form should allow. Their bodies resist damage in ways that seem impossible. Blades that should slice through muscle catch on flesh. Blunt impacts that should shatter bone leave only bruises. Arrows that should pierce organs stop short, embedding shallowly.

This resilience is not armor. It is density. Tenciard flesh is simply more than ordinary flesh, packed tighter, bound stronger, suffused with magic that refuses to yield. A Tenciard weighs more than a person of similar size should, their body denser than appearances suggest.

Beyond durability, Tenciards display heightened vitality. They recover from wounds faster than others of their kind. Poisons and diseases that would fell normal folk are fought off with remarkable speed. They tire slowly and recover quickly. Some Tenciards have been documented fighting for extended periods without rest, sustained by whatever force courses through their blood.

When amplified through focus, these abilities intensify dramatically. A Tenciard pushing their gift to its limits becomes nearly impossible to harm through conventional means. Weapons bounce off skin. Falls that should kill leave them winded but whole. This amplification is exhausting to maintain, but in short bursts it can mean the difference between death and survival.

### Magical Resistance

Most significantly, Tenciards possess remarkable resistance to magic itself. This resistance surpasses even that of Venadants, who are renowned for their ability to deflect magical force. Spells that should bind a Tenciard dissipate upon contact. Curses that should take hold find no purchase. Greschian sorceries, Arcanist workings, Sigilist runes, even the offensive magics of witches and warlocks struggle to affect a Tenciard's body.

This resistance is not absolute, but it is formidable. Magic that would overwhelm or kill a normal target might merely inconvenience a Tenciard. Sustained magical assault can eventually overcome their defenses, but the effort required is substantial. Many mages, upon realizing they face a Tenciard, choose to flee rather than expend themselves against such resistance.

Unlike Venadants, Tenciards can still receive beneficial magic, though with reduced effectiveness. Healing spells work on Tenciard flesh, but slower and weaker than they would on ordinary patients. Blessings of strength or protection can take hold, but provide diminished benefit. The resistance that protects them also filters what they receive. A Tenciard supported by allied mages gains less from that support than another warrior might.

### Differences from Venadants

Tenciards and Venadants share certain traits: innate magical constitution, resistance to spells, bodies that operate beyond normal limits. Some scholars believe Venadants descended from Tenciard bloodlines, the gift diluted and altered through generations of human breeding. Others argue the similarities are coincidental, two separate expressions of similar magical principles.

The differences are substantial. Venadants possess heightened senses that Tenciards entirely lack: sharpened sight, keen smell, predatory instincts, reaction times that border on precognition. A Venadant can smell magic in the air, track prey across miles, react to threats before conscious thought catches up. Tenciards possess none of these abilities. Their senses are no sharper than any ordinary member of their race.

In exchange, Tenciards possess greater raw durability and magical resistance. A Venadant deflects some magical force. A Tenciard shrugs off considerably more. A Venadant is hard to catch and hard to kill. A Tenciard can be caught easily enough but proves far harder to put down once cornered.

Venadants are incapable of using any magic whatsoever. Tenciards face no such restriction. A Tenciard can learn Sigilism, study Arcanist practices, or bind themselves to magical circles without interference. Their innate constitution does not prevent them from channeling external power. Many Tenciards choose to focus on martial pursuits rather than magical study, finding their natural gifts more reliable, but the option remains open to them.

### Limitations

Tenciard abilities offer no external power. A Tenciard cannot hurl force, summon creatures, or manipulate the world around them. Their magic begins and ends at their body. In a world where mages rain fire and call lightning, a Tenciard can only endure and close the distance.

The magical resistance that protects them also diminishes beneficial effects. Healing magic works poorly. Enhancement spells provide reduced benefit. A Tenciard fighting alongside mage-supported allies gains less from that support than others might.

Additionally, Tenciard abilities cannot be suppressed. The magic is always present. This makes them identifiable to those who know what to look for: their unusual weight, the way blades catch on their skin, the resistance felt when grabbing or restraining them. A Tenciard cannot hide what they are from anyone who touches them or strikes them.

### Social Standing

Among dwarves, Tenciards occupy positions of martial honor. They serve as guards, champions, and front-line warriors. Their ability to hold ground, endure punishment, and outlast any opponent makes them invaluable in warfare.

Among Gigamar, Tenciards are less remarkable. The race already possesses considerable physical resilience, and Tenciard Gigamar are simply viewed as particularly robust examples of their kind. They are still valued as warriors, but the distinction carries less weight than it does among dwarves.

Among humans and other races where Tenciards are vanishingly rare, the gift often goes unrecognized. A human Tenciard might simply be considered unusually tough, lucky, or blessed without anyone understanding the true nature of their resilience.

### The Compounding Warriors

Those rare dwarves born with both Gravon and Tenciard abilities become something extraordinary. By increasing their gravitational pull, they amplify all magic within their field, including their own Tenciard constitution. This amplified resilience allows them to withstand even greater gravitational pressure. The effect compounds, each ability feeding the other in an escalating spiral.

At the height of their power, these compounding warriors become nearly unstoppable. Enemies cannot approach them. The gravity crushes those who draw near while the warrior themselves stands unaffected, their Tenciard resilience enhanced by their own Gravon field. Weapons shatter against flesh grown denser than stone.

These dual-gifted individuals are vanishingly rare. Most dwarven kingdoms count themselves fortunate to have even one in a generation.

### Trained Potential

Humans and other races who manifest Tenciard abilities begin at a disadvantage. Their bodies lack the natural resilience that dwarves and Gigamar possess before the magic even manifests. A human Tenciard, untrained, is merely tougher than an ordinary human. A dwarven or Gigamar Tenciard, untrained, is already considerably more formidable.

Yet the magic responds to focus and training. A human who discovers their Tenciard nature and dedicates themselves to developing it can push their abilities far beyond their initial limits. Through years of practice, learning to amplify their gift at will and sustain that amplification for longer periods, a human Tenciard can achieve durability rivaling that of their dwarven and Gigamar counterparts.

This path is neither quick nor easy. It demands constant effort, deliberate exposure to harm in controlled circumstances, and the discipline to push through exhaustion. Many human Tenciards never realize their full potential, living their lives as merely "unusually tough" without understanding what they could become. Those few who commit fully to developing their gift become something remarkable: humans who can stand beside dwarves and Gigamar as equals in resilience, their weaker starting point overcome through sheer dedication.

Some dwarven trainers have taken human Tenciards as students, curious to see how far the gift can be pushed in lesser bodies. The results have surprised even skeptics. The magic, it seems, cares nothing for the flesh it inhabits. It only asks whether the bearer is willing to grow.
