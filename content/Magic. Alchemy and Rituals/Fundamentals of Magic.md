## Scholarly Theories on Magical Inheritance
Scholars have long observed that magical ability runs in families, yet follows no predictable rules. A powerful mage might sire a dozen children, and only one might manifest the gift. Two magically inert parents might produce a child of exceptional potency. The patterns defy simple inheritance.
The prevailing theory holds that magical potential is carried in the blood itself, diluted or concentrated through generations. When two bloodlines merge through marriage, their magical essences combine. Sometimes they reinforce each other, producing strong practitioners. Sometimes they cancel out, producing nothing. Sometimes they mix in unexpected ways, creating abilities neither parent possessed.
Some scholars propose that this inheritance extends beyond immediate parents. A child inherits not just from mother and father but from grandparents, great-grandparents, and lines stretching back generations. According to this view, magical potential can skip generations entirely, lying dormant for centuries before suddenly manifesting. A family with no mages for ten generations might produce a prodigy because some distant ancestor's essence finally found the right combination of blood to awaken.
This would explain why certain families pursue marriages with bloodlines that have not intersected with their own for many generations. Previously unmixed essences, though dormant in each line, might combine in ways neither carried alone, producing abilities no ancestor ever displayed.
This explains why ancient magical families obsessively track their genealogies. They search for patterns in who married whom, which unions produced mages, which produced failures. Some families have identified favorable pairings across generations. Others have discovered bloodlines to avoid, combinations that consistently produce magically inert offspring.
Yet even the most thorough genealogical records cannot predict outcomes with certainty. The blood carries potential, but something else determines whether that potential awakens
	

# Materials of Magic
### Categories of Interaction

Scholars have long observed that certain substances interact with magical energies in predictable ways. Through centuries of experimentation, cataloguing, and sometimes fatal trial and error, practitioners have identified five primary categories of interaction:

**Stabilizers:** Substances that allow practitioners to control volatile or chaotic magical energies that would otherwise be impossible to shape. A stabilizer does not add power. It makes existing power usable. Without the appropriate stabilizer, some magics slip through a practitioner's grasp no matter their skill, like trying to hold water in open fingers. The stabilizer provides the vessel.

**Amplifiers:** Substances that increase the raw magical power available to a practitioner. Scholars describe this as the substance "ringing in sympathy" with the magic being worked, adding its voice to the practitioner's own. The result is greater output than the practitioner could achieve alone.

**Dampeners:** Substances that absorb, scatter, or ground out magical energy. Dampeners reduce or negate magical effects in their presence. Some dampeners affect all magic indiscriminately. Others target specific types while leaving the rest untouched.

**Conductors:** Substances that allow magical energy to flow through them with minimal resistance. Conductors are useful for extending magical effects across distances, creating sympathetic links between targets, or channeling power from one point to another without loss, usually seen in enchanted inks for sigilism and arcanists. 

**Containers:** Substances that can hold magical energy in stasis, storing it for later release. A charged container allows a practitioner to draw upon pre-stored power, casting beyond their normal limits or accessing magic prepared hours or days in advance.

These categories describe function, not exclusivity. A single substance may possess multiple properties depending on the magic being worked. Gold stabilizes certain magics while also amplifying them. Quartz contains energy while also conducting it. Understanding which properties a substance offers for which magics is a lifetime's study.

### Organic Materials

Organic materials behave differently than metals and minerals. Where a vein of gold is much the same as any other vein of gold, organic materials vary dramatically based on their source. The bone of a rabbit and the bone of a dragon share little beyond their basic composition. This makes organic materials simultaneously the most versatile and the most unpredictable category of magical substances.

commonly organic materials are containers and then amplifiers.

**Bone**

Bone is highly dependent on its origin. Scholars have observed that bone retains something of the creature it came from, some trace of essence that persists long after death. Magic worked through bone resonates with the original creature's nature. The bone of a creature known for strength amplifies magics of strength. The bone of a creature known for swiftness amplifies magics of speed.

Most bones function as containers of wild magic. Breaking them correctly during ritual can release this stored essence, though the results are often erratic and unpredictable. Practitioners who work with bone accept a degree of chaos in exchange for power they could not otherwise access.

Rare bones behave differently. Some act as stabilizers, calming volatile magics rather than adding to them. Others function as dampeners, suppressing magical effects in their presence. What determines whether a bone contains, stabilizes, or dampens remains poorly understood. Source creature, age at death, manner of death, conditions of preservation, all have been proposed as factors. None fully explain the variation.

Ancestral bone holds particular significance. Bone from one's own bloodline amplifies magic more strongly than bone from strangers, as though the shared essence creates natural sympathy. This is why some families preserve the bones of their dead rather than burning or burying them, passing down ancestral implements across generations.

Monster bone carries traces of the monster's nature. This makes it useful for practitioners seeking to work magics aligned with that nature, and dangerous for those who do not understand what they handle.

**Blood**

Fresh blood connects directly to the creature it came from. Magic worked through blood affects the source, creating a link that distance does not easily sever. This makes blood one of the most potent conductors known, and one of the most dangerous.

Blood amplifies as well as conducts. Rituals involving multiple Greschian sorcerers pooling their blood produce results none could achieve alone. An Aurian priestess healing through blood contact works more effectively than one healing at a distance. Willingly given blood amplifies more strongly than blood taken by force, for reasons scholars attribute to the giver's intent resonating alongside the physical substance.

Blood loses potency rapidly once separated from its source. Within hours, fresh blood becomes merely dead tissue, useful for nothing. This limitation shapes how blood magic is practiced, requiring immediate use or careful preservation.

In rare cases, blood can be crystallized through alchemical processes, preserving it indefinitely. Crystallized blood behaves almost as an entirely different material. Where fresh blood conducts and amplifies, crystallized blood stabilizes, particularly for blood-based magics. The transformation from liquid to crystal fundamentally alters its magical properties.

**Ivory, Horn, and Fang**

Like bone, ivory, horn, and fang carry traces of their source creature's nature. Items made from powerful beasts amplify magics aligned with that beast's strengths. A wand carved from griffin talon amplifies differently than one carved from serpent fang. This is why horn and ivory from certain creatures commands extraordinary prices among practitioners.

Unlike common bone, ivory and horn are notably less erratic. Where bone often releases wild magic unpredictably, ivory and horn provide steadier amplification. They are less potent than bone at its peak, but far more reliable. A practitioner working with ivory knows roughly what to expect. A practitioner working with bone accepts uncertainty.

This reliability makes ivory and horn valuable even when equivalent bone would be more powerful in ideal circumstances. Many practitioners prefer consistent moderate amplification over inconsistent strong amplification, particularly when working magics where unpredictable results could prove fatal.

**Wood**

Wood is among the most versatile materials available to practitioners. Living wood still connected to its tree can channel nature-based magics with exceptional clarity, the tree itself serving as an extension of the practitioner's working.

Cut wood retains some of this property but fades over time. This aging changes the wood's magical characteristics, though usually within a range related to the original tree's nature. What the tree was in life influences what its wood does in death, even as potency diminishes.

Different trees offer different properties entirely. A staff carved from one species behaves nothing like a staff carved from another. Practitioners who work extensively with wood learn the natures of dozens of species, selecting materials to match their intentions. This variety, combined with wood's availability and ease of shaping, makes it a staple material for foci despite its lower potency compared to metals and gems.

### Metals

Metals function primarily as stabilizers, and secondarily as conductors. Scholars have observed that metals are particularly useful for controlling bounded magics with highly amplified origins, such as Greschian Sorceries or Waldery witcheries. These magical traditions draw from sources so potent that practitioners often struggle to shape the raw power flooding through them. Metal allows one to control this surge with far greater precision. The difference, as one academy instructor famously described it, is like attempting to cup water with bare hands versus using a bucket.

**Gold**

Gold functions as both stabilizer and amplifier for magics pertaining to health, vigor, and blood. Healing magics and arts of rejuvenation work more effectively when channeled through gold. Gold integrated into flesh becomes part of the practitioner permanently. Unlike other metals, gold does not slowly poison or degrade tissue. This permanence makes it ideal for lifelong magical enhancement, though removal becomes impossible without severe damage.

The largest problem with grafting gold into one's body is purity. Gold of sufficient purity to truly avoid toxicity over time is exceedingly rare, beyond the reach of all but the wealthiest individuals. Even those who can afford the finest gold often discover, years or decades later, that impurities accumulated within their flesh. What seemed a permanent enhancement becomes a slow death.

**Silver**

Silver occupies a unique position among metals. It functions as both stabilizer and dampener, a rare combination. Most metals that stabilize do not dampen. Silver does both.

Silver has long been used to counter magicians and certain monsters, though its dampening effect is not as significant as dedicated dampening materials. Where silver excels is in its universality. It stabilizes all manner of magic, regardless of tradition or origin. However, this universal application comes at the cost of potency. Silver's stabilizing effect on any specific magical art is weaker than metals attuned specifically to that art.

Certain magical traditions make explicit use of silver where its properties shine. The magics taught to the Oathbound inner covenant of the Myrkvargr rely heavily on silver implements and adornments. The stabilizing aspect of silver also explains its dampening function. Magics that require a certain amount of chaotic power to function properly find themselves calmed by silver's presence, their necessary volatility smoothed into uselessness.

Silver governs magics pertaining to nullification and cleansing.

**Copper**

Copper serves as a stabilizer for many Noxificers. Scholars have observed that copper's tendency to react with skin on a small scale, constantly and subtly, seems to help Noxificers regulate their abilities over time. The metal becomes a kind of training tool, its persistent minor reactions teaching the body to manage more significant toxic interactions.

Beyond Noxificers, copper proves useful for healers who deal regularly with poisons and toxins. A copper implement aids in drawing out venom, neutralizing harmful substances, and stabilizing patients whose bodies have been corrupted by toxic magic.

Copper governs magics pertaining to poison and toxins.

**Thallium**

Thallium is an extremely rare material that functions as a powerful amplifier. Noxificers who can acquire it shape it into pellets for consumption during times of desperate need. Swallowing a thallium pellet is a near-death sentence for anyone who is not a Noxificer. Even for a Noxificer, such an act is daunting and physically taxing, risking permanent damage even as it grants tremendous temporary power.

The metal is so potent that simply holding it amplifies a Noxificer's abilities significantly. If thallium were not so soft in nature, it would be among the finest materials for crafting Noxificer foci. Its malleability makes it unsuitable for implements that must endure use over time.

Whether thallium can stabilize magics other than poison remains unknown. The metal is too dangerous and too rare for extensive experimentation. As far as scholars have determined, it governs poison alone.

**Lead**

Lead serves as a stabilizer for gravity magic. Gravons working under high strain find that lead helps them maintain control, preventing their fields from spiraling beyond their capacity to manage.

However, lead carries significant risk. Those who use it for gravity magic seem to run heightened danger of Ma'diya. Whether this is due to lead's toxic nature affecting the mind, or some interaction between lead and the fundamental forces Gravons manipulate, scholars have not determined. Many Gravons avoid lead entirely despite its benefits, unwilling to trade stability for sanity.

Lead governs gravity magic.

**Brass**

Brass functions as a stabilizer for practitioners who utilize sound-based magics. Bards, singers who manifest wild magics through their voices, and similar practitioners find brass invaluable for controlling their abilities.

Brass is most commonly encountered in mundane applications that border on magical. Banner holders and announcers use enchanted brass instruments to carry sound clearly across stadiums and battlefields. A brass horn properly crafted can project a voice further and clearer than any natural shout. The introduction of Sigilism and Arcanist practices in recent times has significantly increased the use of brass, as practitioners have developed new techniques for enhancing its natural properties.

Brass governs magics pertaining to sound.

**Bronze**

Bronze serves as a stabilizer for practitioners of body-based magics. Interestingly, Venadants and Tenciards do not require any stabilizer for their innate abilities. Their magic is naturally integrated with their flesh. However, when such practitioners attempt to learn additional magical arts, using bronze as a conduit provides them something to lean upon, a stable foundation from which to work unfamiliar power.

Beyond this specific application, bronze is notably resistant to magical influence. This property makes it useful for crafting magically resistant armaments, shields, and armor that shed hostile spells more readily than steel or iron. Some scholars speculate this is why the ancient giant walls of forgotten times were constructed from a unique bronze alloy. Whatever civilization raised those walls understood that bronze would endure not merely weather and siege, but magical assault as well.

Bronze governs magics pertaining to the body.

### Stones and Minerals
Stones and minerals function primarily as amplifiers and conductors. Where metals stabilize, minerals magnify. A practitioner working through a quality gem can channel power that would overwhelm them if attempted through flesh alone.

Useful minerals are rare. Truly useful minerals of sufficient quality are rarer still. Unlike metals, where purity is the primary concern, minerals demand attention to cut, clarity, shape, and internal structure. A flawed gem may conduct erratically, amplifying in bursts rather than steadily. A poorly cut stone may scatter power rather than focus it. The Scharlite gems of Mar'gat demonstrate this principle starkly: the cut and quality of a Schar-Sorcerer's gem literally defines the upper limit of power they can wield. A master with a flawed stone may find themselves outmatched by an apprentice carrying a perfect one.

This sensitivity to form makes minerals the domain of specialist craftsmen. A goldsmith can work with gold of varying purity and produce serviceable results. A gem-cutter working with magical minerals must achieve near-perfection or risk producing something useless, or worse, dangerous.

**Scharlite**

Scharlite gems are carnelian-kin stones found exclusively within the Vermilion Mount of Mar'gat. They serve as both amplifier and container for those bound to the mountain's secret bounded circle. Only those who undergo the binding ritual can manipulate Scharlite for sorcery. To anyone else, they are merely beautiful red stones.

A Schar-Sorcerer's power depends entirely on their gem. The complexity, size, cut, and raw quality of their Scharlite determines the ceiling of power they can access. A flawed or poorly cut gem limits a practitioner regardless of their skill. This makes Scharlite among the clearest examples of how mineral quality directly constrains magical potential.

Scharlite governs magics tied to the bounded circle of Mar'gat.

**Myrlith (Ebenlith)**

Myrlith is the human name for what dwarves call Ebenlith. It forms from the petrified corpses of dwarves who have died, their bodies hardening into stone denser than any natural mineral. Dwarves never sell Myrlith to outsiders except in extraordinary circumstances. The gift of Myrlith ore to the Myrk Order represents a debt or service of tremendous magnitude.

The stone resonates powerfully with gravity magic. Gravon practitioners who wear Myrlith bands or wield Myrlith weapons can manipulate their equipment's weight at will, making heavy blades light to carry and devastating mid-swing. When forged into weapons and armor, Myrlith behaves more like metal than stone, taking edges and holding shape. It is the preferred material for Gravon armaments.

Among dwarves, Ebenlith is also considered the finest material for Shaping new generations. The dead give form to the young.

Myrlith governs gravity magic.

**Obsidian**

Obsidian is volcanic glass that fractures into edges sharper than any metal blade. It amplifies magics of cutting, severance, and ending. An obsidian knife can sever magical bonds that other materials cannot touch.

Ritual blades for breaking curses, ending bindings, or severing connections are traditionally made from obsidian. The stone's brittleness limits its use for sustained work, but for single decisive cuts, nothing matches it.

Scholars have also noted that obsidian seems to amplify some of the magics wielded by the demons of Pyrgon, their fire and chaotic nature resonating with the volcanic glass. This amplification is limited, but significant enough that obsidian implements are viewed with suspicion in regions that border Pyrgon lands.

Obsidian governs magics of severance, ending, and to a lesser extent, chaotic fire.

**Sapphire**

Sapphire is a favorite among the Arcanists of Lorden. The gem amplifies the geometric circles and triangles that form the foundation of Arcanist practice. Ground into dust, sapphire can be mixed into ink or pressed into carvings, allowing the shapes themselves to conduct and amplify magic.

Many of the lifts and mechanisms within Thangard rely on sapphire-inscribed circles for their function. Training tomes for Arcanist students are written with sapphire-laced ink, making the diagrams within more than mere illustrations. Exceedingly rare are the animated picture books that use sapphire dust to bring their illustrations to brief, flickering life.

Sapphire governs magics of cold and order.

### Purity and Quality

Impurities interfere with a substance's magical properties. Alloyed gold stabilizes less effectively than pure gold. Crystals with inclusions store energy unevenly, sometimes releasing it unpredictably. This is why practitioners pay premium prices for materials of exceptional purity.

Scholars have also observed that materials from certain sources outperform others of identical apparent purity. Gold from one mine may stabilize better than gold from another, though both test as equally pure. The reasons remain debated. Some suggest that conditions during formation matter. depth, pressure, exposure to underground waters or minerals. Others believe certain sources simply produce superior material for reasons not yet understood.
### Sympathetic Pairing

Certain materials work better together than alone. Copper and tin, neither particularly remarkable individually, combine into bronze with properties neither possesses separately. 

Practitioners who understand sympathetic pairing can create implements greater than any single material would allow. This knowledge is jealously guarded by those who possess it, passed from master to apprentice rather than published in common texts.
### Foci and Implements

For substances that cannot be imbued, or for practitioners unwilling to accept the costs, craftsmen create foci: grimoires bound in specific materials, staves capped with conducting metals, orbs of resonant crystal, rings designed to rest against the skin.

The quality of crafting matters. A roughly hewn crystal behaves differently than one cut with precision. A staff assembled carelessly provides less benefit than one constructed by a master. Those who understand the nuances of how shape, polish, and assembly affect a material's magical properties can coax more from a substance than lesser craftsmen ever could.

Master-crafters can further enhance a material's properties through enchantment, binding additional magical effects into the substance itself. This is a domain requiring skills beyond what most practitioners possess, combining material knowledge with magical expertise in ways few ever master.

### Proximity and Effectiveness

Scholars agree that the closer a substance sits to the practitioner's body, the more effectively it interacts with their magic. The hierarchy is well established:

**Implants** embedded within the flesh are most effective. The substance becomes part of the practitioner's physical structure, interacting with their magic as intimately as their own blood and bone.

**Piercings** that pass through flesh approach the effectiveness of implants. The substance sits within the body, not merely against it.

**Worn items** such as rings, necklaces, and bracelets rest against the skin. They provide meaningful benefit but noticeably less than what implants or piercings offer.

**Held implements** such as staves, wands, and orbs must be gripped or carried. The connection is weakest, relying on contact rather than integration.

However, quantity compensates for proximity. A large orb of quality material mounted on a staff can outperform a small implanted piece of the same substance. The greater mass provides interaction that the weaker connection cannot match. This is why battle-mages carry staves capped with fist-sized crystals rather than relying solely on the small gems set into their flesh.

### Imbuing and Its Costs

Not all substances can be safely introduced into the body. Many of the most magically potent materials are toxic to living tissue. Lead, mercury, and certain crystalline compounds offer tremendous magical potential but poison those who attempt to imbue them.

This is why external foci remain common despite being less effective than direct imbuing. A staff can be set down. An implant cannot be removed without surgery. The power of imbuing is real, but so is the price.
