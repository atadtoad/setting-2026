## The Myrkvargr Faith

The Myrkvargr faith is the worship of the Black Wolf, one of the Alderbeasts revered across Lorden. Followers pray to the Black Wolf for protection, strength, and guidance. The faith is open to all who would honor the Wolf, from common farmers seeking safety for their livestock to soldiers asking for courage before battle. Shrines to the Black Wolf can be found throughout Lorden, tended by priests who interpret omens, lead rituals, and preserve the traditions of Wolf-worship.

Most worshippers never advance beyond attending rituals and leaving offerings at shrines. They are Myrkvargr faithful, believers in the Wolf's protection, but they are not of the Covenant.

### Clergy Ranks

**Greytongue (Speaker of Myrk):** Head of the faith and voice of the Black Wolf in mortal matters.

**Greyfather/Greymother (Deacon of Myrk):** Senior clergy who oversee regional shrines and guide multiple congregations. This is the first clergy rank inducted into the Covenant.

**Grey Shepherd (Priest of Myrk):** Leads rituals, interprets omens, and tends to local shrines.

**Grey Robe (Acolyte of Myrk):** Initiates still learning the rites and traditions of the faith.

## The Myrk Order

The Myrk Order is the martial arm of the Myrkvargr faith. Its members, the Myrk Knights, are not ordinary knights. Where a common knight swears fealty to a lord and serves that lord's interests, a Myrk Knight swears first to the Black Wolf and through that oath to the Crown of Lorden. They are warrior-faithful, crusaders of the Black Wolf, templars bound to serve both the throne and the priests of the inner Covenant.

### Myrk Knights and Common Knights

A common knight in Lorden serves his liege lord. His duties are to that lord's lands, that lord's wars, that lord's interests. He may worship any god or none. His oaths are feudal in nature, and when his lord releases him or dies, those oaths may pass to another or end entirely.

A Myrk Knight is different in every respect. He is first and foremost a believer in the Myrkvargr faith who has dedicated his sword to the Black Wolf. His service is not to a local lord but directly to the Crown of Lorden, for the Myrkvargr faith holds that the Black Wolf chose the Thanaric bloodline to rule. A Myrk Knight answers to the King and to the Covenant's priesthood. He guards shrines, hunts those who defile sacred sites, escorts Grey Shepherds through dangerous lands, and stands ready to answer the Greytongue's summons.

Where common knights may be released from service, Myrk Knights serve until death. Where common knights fight for land and title, Myrk Knights fight for the Wolf and the throne it has blessed. They are the sword of the faith, intended from their first oath to one day join the inner Covenant and bind themselves fully to the Black Wolf.

### Order Ranks

**Knight:** A sworn member of the Myrk Order who has completed training and taken oaths to the Wolf and Crown. Not yet of the Covenant.

**Lord Knight:** A Myrk Knight who has been inducted into the Covenant. Lord Knights command chapter houses positioned near every border of Lorden.

**Grand Knight:** The commander of the entire Myrk Order. Sits on the King's Council. Always a member of the Covenant.

## The Myrkvargr Covenant

The Myrkvargr Covenant is the inner circle of the faith. It is not simply a rank or title but a binding, a sacred tie that connects its members directly to the Black Wolf itself. Entry into the Covenant requires a ritual that marks the initiate's soul as belonging to the Wolf. Those who undergo this binding are no longer merely worshippers or servants. They become instruments of the Black Wolf's will in the mortal world.

The Covenant binds together two groups: the Myrk Knights who have proven themselves worthy of advancement to Lord Knight, and the senior clergy who have risen to the rank of Deacon. Both undergo the same ritual. Both bear the same brand. Both carry the Wolf's blessing within them.

Not all who serve the faith join the Covenant. Grey Shepherds tend shrines their entire lives without ever undergoing the binding. Knights of the Myrk Order may serve for decades before being deemed ready. The Covenant is reserved for those called to bear the Wolf's mark and accept its burden.

### The Binding

The Covenant ritual binds the initiate to the Black Wolf itself. What this means precisely, none outside the Covenant can say. Those within speak of feeling the Wolf's presence, of knowing when shrines are defiled even from great distances, of dreams filled with dark forests and yellow eyes watching from shadows. Whether the Black Wolf is a god, an Alderbeast of immense power, or something else entirely, the binding connects Covenant members to it in ways that defy explanation.

This connection grants the Covenant's blessing. In battle, members have been witnessed rising after wounds that should have felled them, continuing to fight when all reason says they should be dead. Yet after such a battle, they are almost always permanently down, as if the Black Wolf grants them one final stand before claiming them.

### The Wolf's Gift

Beyond the blessing of a final stand, those bound to the Covenant undergo changes that set them apart from ordinary men. Their youth lingers far longer than it should. A Covenant member of sixty years may appear no older than forty, their hair untouched by grey, their bodies unbowed by age. They do not live longer than other men, but they remain vital and strong until nearly the end of their days, aging all at once in their final years rather than gradually across a lifetime.

More unsettling still is their physical prowess. Covenant members are stronger and faster than any normal human has a right to be. They move with a swiftness that borders on unnatural, reacting to blows before they land, striking with force that shatters shields and cleaves through armor. In darkness, their senses sharpen. Some claim they can smell fear on an enemy, or hear a heartbeat from across a room. Whether these abilities grow over time or manifest immediately upon binding varies by account.

Those outside the Covenant whisper about what price is paid for such gifts. The Covenant members themselves do not speak of it. They merely serve, stronger and swifter than men should be, until the Wolf claims what it is owed.

### Oath to the Throne

The Covenant is bound by sacred oath to protect the King and the Thanaric bloodline. This duty predates the founding of the Myrk Order itself, stretching back to the earliest days of Lorden when the first Thanaric king supposedly received the Black Wolf's blessing.

Covenant members serve as royal guards and knights of the throne. They follow the King's orders as they would the will of the Black Wolf, for in their belief the two are intertwined. The Black Wolf chose the Thanaric line to rule, and to serve the King is to serve the Wolf.

Lord Knights of the Myrk Order command chapter houses near every border. The Grand Knight sits on the King's Council. In times of war, Covenant knights form the core of the royal host. In times of peace, they guard the throne, patrol the realm, and answer threats too dangerous for common soldiers.

This dual loyalty, to Crown and to Wolf, is considered one and the same by most Covenant members. When the two align, there is no conflict. When they do not, each knight must weigh which oath came first.

### Paths of Entry

**The Sword Path:** Myrk Knights who have served faithfully and proven their devotion may be inducted into the Covenant. This is required before any knight can be considered for Lord Knight. The binding ties the knight not only to the King but to the Black Wolf itself.

**The Faith Path:** Clergy who rise through the ranks of the Myrkvargr priesthood may eventually enter the Covenant, but only upon reaching the rank of Deacon of Myrk. Lower-ranked clergy remain outside the Covenant, serving the faith without bearing the binding. Upon becoming a Greyfather or Greymother, a clergyman undergoes the same ritual as the knights.

### Induction Ritual

Those entering the Covenant, whether knight or clergy, undergo the same rite. The initiate drinks from the Sable Goblet, a sacred vessel blessed by the Greytongue. What the goblet contains is known only to those who have tasted it. Following the drinking, the initiate receives a brand marking their Covenant membership. The location of the brand varies by tradition, though most bear it upon the chest or shoulder.

From the moment of branding, the initiate is bound to the Black Wolf. The connection is immediate. Many describe feeling a presence settle into their bones, a watchfulness that never fully fades. In the days that follow, the physical changes begin. Strength grows. Reflexes sharpen. The Wolf's gift takes hold.

### Covenant Privileges

Members of the Covenant hold the right to call upon fellow Covenant members for aid in times of need. They carry a token or bear the brand identifying them as Covenant, granting them recognition among their brethren. They may speak directly to the Greytongue without intermediary. They are permitted to enter the inner sanctums of Myrkvargr shrines, spaces forbidden to those outside the Covenant, including lower-ranked clergy and common faithful.

### Covenant Responsibilities

Covenant members are sworn to defend all shrines of the Black Wolf, not merely those within their own territory. They must answer the Greytongue's summons in times of crisis, setting aside other duties when called. They perform the rites of passage for new members entering the Covenant. Throughout their lives, they undertake pilgrimages to sacred sites across Lorden. They hunt those who defile Black Wolf shrines, pursuing such offenders until justice is rendered. Above all, they defend the throne and the Thanaric bloodline against all enemies, for the protection of the Crown is inseparable from service to the Wolf.

### Leadership Selection

The Greytongue is chosen through signs and omens interpreted by the Deacons of Myrk. In practice, this selection has remained largely hereditary within a specific bloodline, though the clergy maintain that the omens have simply favored the same blood for generations.

## Beliefs

The Myrkvargr faith holds that the Black Wolf has protected Lorden since before the first Thanaric king took the throne. It grants strength to those who honor it, blessing the faithful in ways both seen and unseen. The Black Wolf is regarded by its followers as the greatest of all Alderbeasts, supreme among the guardians of the land.

Members of the Covenant believe the Black Wolf will one day return in physical form during Lorden's darkest hour, walking among its people once more when they need it most.

### The Living Wolf

Members of the Covenant believe the Black Wolf still walks the land. In what form, they do not know. Some think it takes the shape of an ordinary wolf, watching from treelines. Others believe it moves as a man among men. Some whisper it may be a Monarch entity, akin to those that warlocks bind themselves to. Others reject this comparison as heresy. The truth remains unknown, even to the Greytongue.

## Tensions

Conflict arises when a knight's duty to the King diverges from what they believe the Black Wolf would demand. Some clergy believe the Covenant has become too militarized through its deep ties to the Myrk Order, its spiritual purpose overshadowed by martial concerns. Beyond the Covenant itself, tension exists between those who see the Black Wolf as supreme among Alderbeasts and broader Alderdyr followers who honor all Alderbeasts equally, viewing such claims of supremacy as arrogance.

Additionally, some within the broader Myrkvargr faith question the necessity of the binding. They argue that devotion to the Wolf should not require marking one's soul, that the Covenant has created a division between those who are truly bound and those who merely believe. The Covenant maintains that the binding is necessary for those who would act as the Wolf's instruments, not a judgment upon common faithful.
