## Summary

The Myrkvargr Covenant is the inner circle of those who worship the Black Wolf of Lorden. Unlike the broader Alderdyr faith which remains decentralized and informal, the Covenant is an organized religious tradition with ranks, rites, and formal membership. It binds both knights of the Myrk Order and senior clergy of the Myrkvargr church under shared oaths.

Those inducted into the Covenant are marked by a strange blessing. In battle, Covenant members have been witnessed rising after wounds that should have felled them, continuing to fight when all reason says they should be dead. The source of this resilience remains unknown. Yet after such a battle, they are almost always permanently down, as if the Black Wolf grants them one final stand before claiming them.

## Oath to the Throne

The Covenant is bound by sacred oath to protect the King and the Thanaric bloodline. This duty predates the founding of the Myrk Order itself, stretching back to the earliest days of Lorden when the first Thanaric king supposedly received the Black Wolf's blessing.

Covenant members serve as royal guards and knights of the throne. They follow the King's orders as they would the will of the Black Wolf, for in their belief the two are intertwined. The Black Wolf chose the Thanaric line to rule, and to serve the King is to serve the Wolf.

Lord Knights of the Myrk Order, all of whom are Covenant members, command chapter houses near every border. The Grand Knight sits on the King's Council. In times of war, Covenant knights form the core of the royal host. In times of peace, they guard the throne, patrol the realm, and answer threats too dangerous for common soldiers.

This dual loyalty, to crown and to wolf, is considered one and the same by most Covenant members. When the two align, there is no conflict. When they do not, each knight must weigh which oath came first.

## Paths of Entry

### The Sword Path

Knights of the Myrk Order who wish to advance beyond the rank of Knight must be inducted into the Covenant. This is required before any knight can be considered for Lord Knight. The induction is overseen by church clergy and binds the knight's oath not only to the King but to the Black Wolf itself.

### The Faith Path

Those who serve the Myrkvargr church may rise through its ranks through devotion and service. They tend shrines, interpret omens, and preserve the traditions of the Black Wolf. There is no martial requirement.

Clergy do not join the inner Covenant simply by serving the religious order. Lower ranks remain outside the Covenant proper. Only upon reaching the rank of Deacon of Myrk does a member of the church undergo the same induction ritual as the knights, formally entering the Covenant.

## Church Ranks

The Speaker of Myrk, known as the Greytongue, stands as the head of the faith and voice of the Black Wolf in mortal matters. Below the Greytongue serve the Deacons of Myrk, called Greyfathers or Greymothers, senior clergy who oversee regional shrines and guide multiple congregations. The Deacons are the first rank inducted into the Covenant proper. Beneath them are the Priests of Myrk, known as Grey Shepherds, who lead rituals, interpret omens, and tend to local shrines. At the lowest rank are the Acolytes of Myrk, called Grey Robes, initiates still learning the rites and traditions of the faith.

## Induction Ritual

Those entering the Covenant, whether knight or clergy, undergo the same rite. The initiate drinks from the Sable Goblet, a sacred vessel blessed by the Greytongue. What the goblet contains is known only to those who have tasted it. Following the drinking, the initiate receives a brand marking their Covenant membership. The location of the brand varies by tradition, though most bear it upon the chest or shoulder.

## Covenant Privileges

Members of the Covenant hold the right to call upon fellow Covenant members for aid in times of need. They carry a token or bear the brand identifying them as Covenant, granting them recognition among their brethren. They may speak directly to the Greytongue without intermediary. They are permitted to enter the inner sanctums of Myrkvargr shrines, spaces forbidden to those outside the Covenant.

## Covenant Responsibilities

Covenant members are sworn to defend all shrines of the Black Wolf, not merely those within their own territory. They must answer the Greytongue's summons in times of crisis, setting aside other duties when called. They perform the rites of passage for new members entering the Covenant. Throughout their lives, they undertake pilgrimages to sacred sites across Lorden. They hunt those who defile Black Wolf shrines, pursuing such offenders until justice is rendered. Above all, they defend the throne and the Thanaric bloodline against all enemies, for the protection of the crown is inseparable from service to the Wolf.

## Leadership Selection

The Greytongue is chosen through signs and omens interpreted by the Deacons of Myrk. In practice, this selection has remained largely hereditary within a specific bloodline, though the clergy maintain that the omens have simply favored the same blood for generations.

## Beliefs

The Covenant holds that the Black Wolf has protected Lorden since before the first Thanaric king took the throne. It grants strength to those who honor it, blessing the faithful in ways both seen and unseen. The Black Wolf is regarded as the greatest of all Alderbeasts, supreme among the guardians of the land. Members believe the Black Wolf will one day return in physical form during Lorden's darkest hour, walking among its people once more when they need it most.

## Tensions

Conflict arises when a knight's duty to the King diverges from what they believe the Black Wolf would demand. Some clergy believe the Covenant has become too militarized through its deep ties to the Myrk Order, its spiritual purpose overshadowed by martial concerns. Beyond the Covenant itself, tension exists between those who see the Black Wolf as supreme among Alderbeasts and broader Alderdyr followers who honor all Alderbeasts equally, viewing such claims of supremacy as arrogance.

## The Living Wolf

Members of the Covenant believe the Black Wolf still walks the land. In what form, they do not know. Some think it takes the shape of an ordinary wolf, watching from treelines. Others believe it moves as a man among men. Some whisper it may be a Monarch entity, akin to those that warlocks bind themselves to. Others reject this comparison as heresy. The truth remains unknown, even to the Greytongue.
