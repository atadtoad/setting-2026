# Deities
Worshipped creatures and entities vary greatly in type and nature. Different allometramages and morphalhemists classify them in distinct ways. The most complex and comprehensive of these classifications was written by an old elf of the western world in a tome known as the "Compendia Divinity," with many modern contributions from the Astralegis and the Magis Laboratorium.

## Gods
Gods are creatures or humanoids that have attained divinity, gaining the ability to manipulate one of reality's core pillars: matter, time, space, or otherwise.

No god has ever been recorded as seen in person. They seem to exist through belief rather than physical form, their presence felt only through signs, omens, and the power they grant their followers.

Some gods become gods by first being Godlings, though the difference between the two is heavily debated among Theurgists. The main consideration appears to be lifespan. Godlings are still mortal and can die of old age or by conventional weapon, though killing one is extremely difficult. Gods, according to Theurgists, cannot be slain. Even if destroyed, they seem to revive.

Both Godlings and Gods hunger for a particular type of sustenance: magic, sacrifice, or some other form of supernatural consumption. For some, it is specifically the ears or hearts of humanoids. Others siphon magic, blood, or bones.

Some scholars believe that gods may simply be the Monarchs of certain Bounded Circles, entities ruling over demonic realms whose influence bleeds into the mortal world. This comparison is considered heretical by many religious authorities.
## Godlings

Godlings arise from many origins. Monsters, magbeasts, and beasts have all undergone the transition from creature to divine being. This ascension requires belief, need, and the right circumstances within a populated area. A village might believe a local magbeast protects their hunts and leave it tribute. Over time, the lingering magic within these ritualistic acts enhances the creature, eventually ascending it to Godling status.

Each Godling's nature and power is unique. Yet all seem to share one of two paths to divinity. The first is surviving an insurmountable challenge, something depicted as impossible by nearly all who witness or hear of it. The second is understanding a fundamental truth of the world, a teaching, a revelation granted through means unknown. Without treading one of these paths, no amount of worship alone can cause a seed of godhood to take root and grow within a creature. Some scholars argue otherwise, claiming that worship in extraordinary quantities over long periods may stir something within the soul, forcing divinity to manifest. If true, this path is far rarer than the others.
## Demi-Gods

Demi-gods are only partially divine. They are not necessarily weaker or stronger than Godlings. The status of demi-god is typically gained either at birth or through a special feat or ritual that grants partial divinity.

Those born as demi-gods tend to be weaker than Godlings. However, those who earn their divinity through deeds or ritual often surpass Godlings in power.

## Alderbeasts

Alderbeasts are Magbeasts that have achieved power equivalent to Godlings. The concept is the same, simply a different term used when a Magbeast attains such status. In Lorden, these ascended creatures are called Alderbeasts. The people pray to them and leave ritualized offerings in their honor.
